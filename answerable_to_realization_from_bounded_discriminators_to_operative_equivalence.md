# Answerable to Realization: From Bounded Discriminators to Operative Equivalence

## Abstract

Mathematics may legitimately begin with an equivalence relation:

\[
\sim\;\subseteq R\times R.
\]

It may then form equivalence classes and the quotient:

\[
R/\!\sim.
\]

Nothing in that formal construction requires repair.

A different starting point is required when the relation is presented as describing a realized measurement, perception, computation, classification, representation, concept, procedure, or action.

In that setting, the operative primitive is not initially a given equivalence relation.

It is a realized bounded discriminator: an organized system coupled to a target through paths that preserve some differences, eliminate others, and produce operative states with finite resolution, finite stability, finite duration, and finite accessibility.

Let a realized arrangement generate a discrimination mapping:

\[
\Phi:R\to D.
\]

The mapping induces:

\[
r_1\sim_{\Phi}r_2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Phi(r_1)=\Phi(r_2).
\]

The realizational order is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\text{realized bounded discriminator}
\to
\text{operative mapping}
\to
\text{equivalence relation}
\to
\text{quotient}
}
\]

The relation is answerable to realization because no operative equivalence can be maintained without structure that reliably implements the relevant identification.

A light distinction requires light-sensitive structure.

A sound distinction requires vibration-sensitive structure.

Recognition of human speech requires organization coupled and calibrated to realizations humans can produce.

Preservation of one second of recording requires structure capable of maintaining discriminating state across at least that horizon.

Catching an exception requires a path that distinguishes the exception and redirects operation.

Moving five people vertically through a building requires a realized mechanism capable of supporting and moving them.

The realization is not an optional example added after the relation.

For realized use, it is what makes the relation operative at all.

Because every realized discriminator is bounded and environmentally coupled, perfect discrimination is not ordinarily available. Operative distinctions are maintained reliably enough relative to a task, occasion, and horizon. Approximation, interference, and finite resolution are therefore not external objections to the framework. They are consequences of the bounded realization from which operative equivalence arises.

The same perspective clarifies why data matters, why arbitrary formal distinctions differ from extracted relations, why tools extend discrimination and action, and why formal possibility must be separated from realizational possibility.

The central claim is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{When a formal relation is used to explain a realized phenomenon, it must be answerable to the realized organization that makes it operative.}
}
\]

---

## 1. Two Legitimate Starting Points

There are two distinct and legitimate starting points.

### Formal starting point

```text
set R
+ equivalence relation ~
    ↓
quotient R/~
```

This is sufficient for mathematics.

A theorem about equivalence relations, kernels, congruences, partitions, or quotient objects does not need to reconstruct a physical, biological, computational, or institutional realization.

### Realizational starting point

```text
target realization
+ realized bounded discriminator
+ coupling and propagation paths
    ↓
operative discrimination mapping Phi
    ↓
induced relation ~Phi
    ↓
quotient R/~Phi
```

This starting point becomes relevant when the relation is meant to describe something that is measured, detected, computed, recognized, remembered, classified, acted upon, or maintained by an actual organization.

The realizational account does not replace the formal one.

It explains how a relation becomes operative in a realized system.

---

## 2. The Shift in Primitive

The central shift can be stated compactly.

The formal construction begins:

\[
\boxed{\text{Given an equivalence relation }\sim}
\]

The realizational construction begins:

\[
\boxed{\text{Given a realized bounded discriminator}}
\]

The second starting point does not assume that a target arrives already partitioned.

The discriminator is coupled to target states and enters its own operative states.

A mathematical representation of that operation is:

\[
\Phi:R\to D.
\]

Equality of operative outcomes induces equivalence:

\[
r_1\sim_{\Phi}r_2
\iff
\Phi(r_1)=\Phi(r_2).
\]

The quotient follows:

\[
R/\!\sim_{\Phi}.
\]

The map is not an unexplained object floating between the realization and the relation.

It represents what the realized discriminator does.

## 3. The Discriminator Is Not Merely the Map

A map is a mathematical object.

A realized discriminator is an organized realization.

It may include:

```text
sensitive elements
propagation paths
transduction mechanisms
state transitions
memory
calibration
energy supply
error handling
maintenance
output paths
interaction history
```

The mapping:

\[
\Phi:R\to D
\]

is a formal representation of the distinctions made available by that organization under specified conditions.

The discriminator constrains the mapping because its realized organization is the mapping's operative basis.

Asking what constrains the image formed by a camera is answered by examining:

```text
its optics
its sensor
its exposure
its circuitry
its calibration
its noise environment
its output path
```

These are not additional constraints imposed on a fully formed abstract camera mapping.

They are the realization through which the mapping exists.

Likewise, asking what constrains a realized equivalence relation is answered by identifying the organization that preserves and merges the relevant distinctions.

---

## 4. No Operative Equivalence Without Implementing Structure

Suppose a system is said to treat two target states as equivalent.

For that statement to describe realized operation, something must make the two states produce the same operative result.

There must be structure that:

```text
receives or encounters effects of the target
propagates those effects
transforms them
maintains relevant state
produces an operative outcome
```

Without such structure, there is no realized identification.

There may still be a formally defined relation.

But there is no operative equivalence in the system under discussion.

This yields a no-free-discrimination principle:

> No realized distinction or identification is available without realized organization capable of producing and maintaining it.

The same applies to action.

No elevator function exists in a building merely because vertical transport can be formally described.

A mechanism must support the load, produce force, constrain motion, receive commands, and maintain safe operation.

No exception is caught merely because an exception class can be named.

A path must discriminate the exception and redirect computation.

No one-second recording exists merely because one second can be represented mathematically.

Some state must remain available across the required interval.

---

## 5. Simple Constraints Are Often Sufficient

The constraints needed to explain a discriminator are often direct.

If light distinctions must become operative, introduce light-sensitive elements.

If sound distinctions must become operative, introduce elements sensitive to vibration or pressure variation.

If human speech is to be recognized, the discriminator must be coupled and calibrated to patterns humans can produce and to the temporal structure through which those patterns unfold.

If at least one second of recording must remain available, the organization must preserve differentiated state for at least one second and provide a path through which that state can later affect operation.

If five people must be moved vertically through a building, the organization must include a mechanism with sufficient space, support, force, control, and stability.

If an exception must be caught, computation must include a distinction-producing path capable of recognizing that condition and selecting an alternative continuation.

These constraints are not difficult because the task already specifies relevant consequences.

A realized arrangement must contain structure capable of producing those consequences.

There is no need to add an independent metaphysical source of constraint beyond the realization.

The realization is the constraint.

---

## 6. Why the Relation Is Answerable to Realization

A formal equivalence relation may be chosen for any mathematically legitimate reason.

A realized equivalence relation is answerable to the organization that implements it.

To say that a relation is answerable to realization means at least:

```text
its distinctions must be available through realized coupling
its identifications must follow from realized operation
its stability must be supported over the relevant horizon
its outputs must be accessible to the organization using them
its reliability must be sufficient for the operative continuation
```

A relation that cannot be produced, maintained, or used by the proposed organization does not describe that organization's operative equivalence.

This does not make the relation absolute.

Different realizations may induce different relations on the same target.

An ordinary camera, thermal camera, microphone, retina, legal procedure, and numerical representation preserve different differences.

Their relations are relative but not arbitrary.

They are constrained by what their realizations can sustain.

---

## 7. Reliability Is Part of Realization

An operative distinction need not be perfect.

It must be reliable enough for the operation under analysis.

The phrase `reliable enough` restores several suppressed indices:

```text
reliable enough for which continuation
under which environmental conditions
across which occasions
for how long
with which tolerated error
at which cost
```

A discriminator may preserve a distinction under laboratory conditions and lose it under heat, noise, motion, or damage.

A memory may preserve a state for milliseconds but not for a day.

A classifier may distinguish clear speech but not heavily distorted speech.

A detector may separate two signals when they are far apart but merge them near its resolution limit.

The induced equivalence is therefore indexed to the realized arrangement and its maintained conditions.

One may write:

\[
\sim_{A,P,O,H,E},
\]

where the indices may represent organization, paths, occasions, horizon, and evaluation or continuation conditions.

For ordinary use, these indices can be suppressed.

For explanation, failure analysis, or comparison, they can be restored.

## 8. Approximation Is Not an External Objection

A concern about approximate equivalence may initially appear to challenge the simple relation:

\[
r_1\sim_{\Phi}r_2
\iff
\Phi(r_1)=\Phi(r_2).
\]

But approximation is itself generated by realized boundedness.

Every realized discriminator has limits arising from conditions such as:

```text
finite resolution
finite sampling
finite memory
finite energy
finite bandwidth
finite interaction time
material variation
thermal fluctuation
background interference
unstable coupling
maintenance failure
```

Absolute perfection in distinguishability is not ordinarily available to a bounded realized system.

The framework therefore does not need to assume an infinitely precise microphysical map.

It can distinguish several levels.

### Microrealizational variation

The underlying physical state may vary on every occasion.

### Stable operative outcome

A decision, symbol, reading, action, or maintained state may nevertheless remain the same across that variation.

### Induced operative equivalence

Target states are equivalent when they reliably lead to the same operative outcome at the selected level and horizon.

Thus strict equivalence can appear at the level of stabilized outcomes even when their lower-level realizations vary.

If no stable outcome is produced, the appropriate structure may instead be:

```text
confusability
similarity
tolerance
probabilistic overlap
graded discrimination
```

The framework should not force every imperfect discrimination into an exact quotient.

But where a realized system eventually selects the same operative state, exact equivalence can still be induced at that operative level.

## 9. The Environment Is Inside the Realized Arrangement

Interference does not arrive from a metaphysical outside.

A realized discriminator is always situated in an environment.

Its operation depends on maintained differences between:

```text
target effects
background effects
internal variation
stored state
incoming energy
outgoing consequences
```

The effective mapping is therefore not determined by an isolated device alone.

It is generated by the device in coupling with relevant surroundings.

One may represent this as:

\[
\Phi_{A,P,E_n,H}:R\to D,
\]

where \(E_n\) includes environmental conditions relevant to operation.

Changing the environment may change which distinctions are preserved.

A microphone in a quiet room and the same microphone beside a jet engine need not induce the same operative partition of speech events.

A visual discriminator in daylight and the same discriminator in darkness do not preserve the same target distinctions.

The environment therefore helps determine the realized mapping without becoming an additional mysterious source of constraint.

It is part of the realized interaction.

## 10. Boundedness Forces Identification

Let:

\[
\Phi:R\to D.
\]

If:

\[
|R|>|D|,
\]

then \(\Phi\) cannot be injective.

There must exist distinct states \(r_1,r_2\in R\) such that:

\[
\Phi(r_1)=\Phi(r_2).
\]

At least one non-singleton equivalence class is unavoidable.

This is the elementary cardinality case.

Realized boundedness is broader.

Even when formal state spaces have equal cardinality, the realized organization may fail to preserve every difference because of:

```text
limited resolution
limited energy
limited time
partial observability
noise
unstable memory
restricted access
finite maintenance
```

Boundedness forces some loss or merging of distinctions.

It does not by itself determine which distinctions are merged.

The realized organization, coupling, history, and horizon determine the particular mapping.

---

## 11. The Realization Constrains the Particular Classes

The relation is not selected by boundedness alone.

A retina, microphone, thermometer, parser, debugger, database index, and elevator controller are all bounded.

But they do not induce the same equivalence classes.

Their structures are sensitive to different effects and maintain different states.

The particular quotient follows from the particular realization.

This can be summarized as:

```text
boundedness
    → some identification is unavoidable

realized organization
    → these identifications occur
```

The question:

> What additional constraints determine \(\Phi\)?

has a direct answer:

> The realized discriminator and its maintained coupling determine \(\Phi\).

Further detail is required only when the description of the discriminator is too coarse for the explanatory question.

There is no requirement to enumerate every molecule, transistor, institutional rule, or environmental fluctuation.

An explanation stops at the level sufficient to account for the distinction under investigation.

## 12. There Is No Infinite Regress of Constraints

Making the generator explicit does not require explaining all of reality before using a quotient.

An analysis may begin with a realized bounded discriminator as its realizational primitive.

For one question, `camera` may be sufficient.

For another, one must distinguish:

```text
lens geometry
sensor type
exposure
spectral response
noise floor
calibration
```

For another, the manufacturing history or environmental damage may matter.

The level of decomposition depends on which operative distinction is being explained.

This is not arbitrariness.

It is explanatory selectivity.

The same occurs throughout science and engineering.

A bridge may be treated as a load-bearing structure in one analysis and as a detailed material system in another.

A processor may be treated through its instruction set in one analysis and through transistor behavior in another.

The framework requires that the selected level remain answerable to realization.

It does not require that every explanation descend without limit.

## 13. Data Makes Formal Relations Answerable to Realized Interaction

Why do scientific papers seek real data?

Because data constrains which distinctions and relations can responsibly be attributed to a realized phenomenon.

Without realized interaction, one may define countless elegant partitions, correlations, similarities, classifications, or invariants.

Most will have no demonstrated bearing on the target.

Data provides realized traces generated through:

```text
measurement
sampling
recording
transmission
storage
preprocessing
```

A relation extracted from data is not therefore infallible or absolute.

It remains answerable to:

```text
the target interaction
the instrument
the sampling path
the recording conditions
the analysis procedure
the selected horizon
```

But it is not unconstrained in the way a freely invented relation can be.

The data places resistance on interpretation.

It rules out some claims, supports others, and reveals where the realized discriminator failed to preserve needed distinctions.

## 14. Extracted Relations and Arbitrary Character Sequences

Consider two strings with identical formal structure.

One is generated by arbitrary character concatenation.

The other is recorded from DNA, speech, telemetry, source code, or a measuring instrument.

As strings, both can be studied mathematically.

Their formal properties may be identical.

Their explanatory status differs because one is coupled to a realized generator of interest.

The important difference is not located inside the letter shapes alone.

It lies in the generative path.

A sequence extracted from a realized process may preserve distinctions produced by that process.

An arbitrary concatenation has no such demonstrated dependency.

Likewise, two equivalence relations may be formally isomorphic while differing radically in scientific relevance.

One may be freely stipulated.

The other may be generated by a realized measurement or operation.

The second is answerable to the path that produced it.

This is why causal and historical provenance matters.

## 15. Formal Interest and Realizational Relevance

Pure mathematics may legitimately study arbitrary structures.

A formally elegant equivalence relation need not be extracted from data to deserve mathematical attention.

Its value may lie in:

```text
proof
consistency
structure
compression
connection
counterexample
formal possibility
```

The realizational demand begins only when the structure is used to describe or explain a realized phenomenon.

Then elegance alone does not establish relevance.

A classification of organisms, a model of perception, a medical distinction, a measurement scale, or a computational abstraction must remain answerable to realized evidence and operation.

The distinction is therefore not:

```text
useful mathematics
versus
useless mathematics
```

It is:

```text
formal legitimacy
versus
realizational adequacy
```

A relation may possess the first without yet possessing the second.

## 16. Measurement Does Not Discover a Pre-Partitioned Target

A measuring instrument does not receive equivalence classes already attached to the target.

It receives effects through a coupling path.

Let:

\[
\Phi_M:R\to D_M.
\]

Then:

\[
r_1\sim_M r_2
\iff
\Phi_M(r_1)=\Phi_M(r_2).
\]

The measurement classes are generated by what the instrument can reliably preserve.

A higher-resolution instrument may refine the relation:

\[
\sim_{M_2}\subseteq\sim_{M_1}.
\]

The new instrument does not merely add labels to a fixed partition.

It introduces organization through which previously merged target differences can produce different operative states.

## 17. Microscopes, Telescopes, and Thermal Cameras

A microscope is not invented to constrain unaided human vision further.

It is introduced because unaided visual organization merges distinctions that become consequential.

The composite arrangement:

```text
human + microscope
```

preserves differences that the unaided human arrangement does not.

A telescope similarly changes the available coupling to distant targets.

A thermal camera introduces sensitivity to differences largely unavailable through ordinary visible-light coupling.

These tools do not eliminate boundedness.

They reorganize it.

They refine some equivalence classes while possibly coarsening or ignoring others.

A thermal camera may preserve temperature differences while merging visible-surface differences.

A microscope may preserve fine spatial differences over a small field while sacrificing broad contextual access.

Extension is therefore selective rather than absolute.

## 18. Monitors and Access to Machine State

Why use a monitor when typing?

Why not simply remember what was typed and infer how the system must have changed?

Because the human's internal state is not identical to the machine's current state.

Typing introduces effects into a system through paths that may include:

```text
input buffering
editing
validation
program execution
state transitions
errors
latency
storage
network interaction
```

Memory of intended input does not preserve every distinction produced downstream.

A monitor creates an output path through which machine state can affect the human discriminator.

The composite arrangement:

```text
human
+ keyboard
+ machine
+ display path
```

supports distinctions that the human alone cannot maintain.

The monitor does not merely decorate computation.

It couples otherwise inaccessible state back into operative human discrimination.

## 19. Exception Handling as Realized Discrimination

An exception is not caught merely because a programmer can name its type.

A realized computation must include structure that:

```text
detects the relevant condition
preserves its identity
interrupts or redirects the current path
selects an alternative continuation
```

Without that structure, the exception may occur without becoming operationally distinct from generic failure or termination.

The relevant equivalence classes are implemented by the program and runtime organization.

States that produce the same continuation are equivalent relative to that handling path.

Introducing a catch mechanism refines the operative partition of failure conditions.

It makes a previously merged condition available for differentiated continuation.

## 20. Memory Is a Temporal Discriminator

To distinguish only the present moment requires less organization than to distinguish a temporally extended sequence.

Suppose a system must recognize a pattern extending across one second.

It must preserve relevant state across that interval.

A purely instantaneous response cannot discriminate sequences whose differences exist only through temporal order.

Memory therefore changes the effective mapping.

Let:

\[
\Phi_0:R_t\to D_0
\]

represent an effectively instantaneous discriminator.

Let:

\[
\Phi_H:R_{[t-H,t]}\to D_H
\]

represent a discriminator with horizon \(H\).

Increasing the maintained horizon may split classes merged by the instantaneous mapping.

A spoken word, melody, movement, exception trace, or causal sequence may become recognizable only when temporally distributed differences remain available together.

## 21. Externalization Enlarges Composite Discrimination

A local discriminator may be bounded while a larger coupled arrangement preserves more distinctions.

Let:

\[
\Phi_L:R\to D_L
\]

be a local mapping.

Let an external system provide:

\[
\Phi_X:R\to D_X.
\]

The composite may be represented as:

\[
\Phi_C(r)
=
(\Phi_L(r),\Phi_X(r)).
\]

The composite can distinguish states merged by either component alone.

Examples include:

```text
writing
records
databases
scientific instruments
monitors
other agents
retrieval systems
environmental traces
```

The enlargement is operative only when the external state remains accessible through maintained coupling.

A forgotten password, unreadable archive, disconnected sensor, or destroyed record does not enlarge current discrimination.

## 22. From Boundedness to Experienced Lack

A realized organization can only encounter absence or limitation relative to distinctions and continuations it can already sustain.

A useful generative pattern is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{realized boundedness}
\to
\text{encountered limitation}
\to
\text{experienced or operative lack}
\to
\text{attempted extension of discrimination or action}
}
\]

This order avoids assuming that a fully formed desire exists before realization.

The organization first exists with particular capacities.

It encounters effects, actions, or outcomes that exceed those capacities.

If it can preserve the contrast between its current operation and another available or imaginable outcome, the limitation may become operative as lack.

The organization may then reorganize itself, recruit tools, coordinate with others, or construct new mechanisms.

The extension does not emerge from desire alone.

The desire itself is realized through memory, comparison, valuation, imagination, and action organization.

---

## 23. Boundedness Does Not Force Every Desire

The relation:

```text
boundedness
    → desire
```

would be too strong.

Humans lack countless capacities they never desire.

A limitation becomes an operative lack only under additional realized conditions such as:

```text
encounter with an alternative
preservation of the contrast
valuation of the alternative
memory of failure or absence
imagination of another continuation
availability of action paths
social transmission
```

Thus boundedness is not sufficient for every desire.

But it is a condition of lack.

One cannot experience the lack of a capacity while already possessing that capacity without relevant limitation.

The desire to throw fireballs, fly unaided, see microscopic organisms, reach distant planets, or calculate beyond unaided memory becomes intelligible against the bounded organization of the human body and its existing tools.

The framework can therefore treat many goals not as unexplained primitives but as later organizations generated within bounded interaction.

## 24. Lightning, Fireballs, and Realized Imagination

A human may see lightning while lacking any bodily mechanism capable of producing a similar effect at will.

The contrast can become operative through:

```text
perception of the effect
memory of the event
recognition of one's inability
valuation of the power or consequence
imaginative recombination
symbolic communication
```

The resulting desire is not a free-floating cause outside realization.

It is itself a realized state of an organization that can preserve a distinction between:

```text
what occurred
what the body can currently do
what another organization might make possible
```

Stories, tools, weapons, electrical systems, and visual effects may then become attempted extensions of action or representation.

The framework does not claim that every fantasy will be realized.

It explains how a bounded organization can generate operative contrasts that later guide construction.

Examples:

## 26. Formal Possibility and Realizational Possibility

The framework requires a distinction between two forms of possibility.

### Formal possibility

An object, relation, operation, or sequence is permitted by a formal system.

### Realizational possibility

A bounded organization can actually construct, maintain, access, or perform it under some realizable conditions.

The two should not be confused.

An equivalence relation may be formally definable without being implemented by any known or possible bounded discriminator under the specified conditions.

A numerical object may be formally characterized even though no physical system can explicitly store all of its information.

A process may be specified through an unbounded idealization even though every actual execution is finite.

Formal possibility is not invalidated by realizational limitation.

Realizational claims simply require more than formal consistency.

## 27. Python Integers and Potential Unboundedness

Python integers are often described as having arbitrary precision.

This does not mean that any realized Python execution contains an actually infinite integer or has performed infinitely many integer operations.

It means that the representation is not fixed in advance to a single small word size in the same way as a bounded-width integer type.

In principle, larger finite integers can be represented as more resources are supplied.

Every realized computation remains bounded by conditions such as:

```text
available memory
address space
execution time
energy
hardware stability
operating-system limits
process lifetime
```

No realized Python computation has produced an integer with infinitely many digits.

No realized execution has completed infinitely many integer calculations.

The useful distinction is:

```text
potentially extensible without a fixed formal bound
versus
actually infinite in a realized execution
```

This is a direct example of why formal descriptions should not be mistaken for realized capacity.

## 28. Actual Infinity Is Not an Operative State of a Bounded Discriminator

A bounded discriminator has only finitely maintained structure at any occasion and across any finite horizon.

It cannot explicitly occupy an operative state containing infinitely many independently discriminated elements.

It may manipulate finite symbols that refer to infinity.

It may prove universal propositions.

It may define infinite sets.

It may implement rules that can be iterated without a fixed formal endpoint.

But the realized token, proof, rule, and computation remain finite organizations.

The distinction between referring to an infinite structure and realizing infinitely many discriminated states is essential.

The framework does not deny infinity in mathematics.

It denies the unnoticed transfer from formal infinity to actually realized discriminative capacity.

## 29. The Map Comes Before the Operative Class

A realized organization need not enumerate an equivalence class before treating its members alike.

It may simply produce the same operative outcome.

The class is recoverable from the operation:

```text
same operative outcome
    ↓
same fiber of Phi
    ↓
same induced equivalence class
```

The mapping is therefore generatively prior to the explicit class.

A speech recognizer need not store a list of every waveform it will classify as the same word.

A retina need not enumerate all photon patterns that produce the same perceptual state.

An exception handler need not list every microphysical machine state that produces the same exception object.

The operative mapping can implement the identification without explicitly representing the full extension of the class.

## 30. Data Extraction Is Also Realized Discrimination

The phrase `extracted from data` should not be treated as magical access to reality.

Extraction is itself performed by a realized organization.

A data pipeline may include:

```text
instrumentation
sampling
encoding
filtering
normalization
feature construction
model fitting
thresholding
human interpretation
```

Each stage preserves some distinctions and merges others.

The final relation is answerable not only to the target but also to the path through which target effects became data.

This does not collapse extraction into arbitrariness.

The path is constrained by realized input and can fail in discoverable ways.

But it means that scientific objectivity is not produced by removing all discriminators.

It is produced by exposing, testing, comparing, calibrating, and maintaining the discriminators through which claims become answerable to realization.

## 31. Reproducibility as Stability of Generated Equivalence

A relation extracted on one occasion may fail to reappear on another.

Reproducibility asks whether relevant distinctions remain stable across changes in:

```text
occasion
operator
instrument
sample
location
implementation
analysis path
```

In the present framework, reproducibility concerns the stability of generated mappings or of their induced partitions.

Let:

\[
\Phi_i:R\to D_i
\]

represent different realized occasions.

One may ask whether:

\[
\sim_{\Phi_i}
\]

remains sufficiently stable across \(i\).

Reproducibility does not require identical microphysical realization.

It requires preservation of the operative distinctions relevant to the claim.

## 32. Objectivity as Cross-Realization Constraint

A relation becomes more than a private or local identification when multiple independently organized paths converge on compatible distinctions.

Suppose:

\[
\Phi_1:R\to D_1,
\qquad
\Phi_2:R\to D_2,
\qquad
\Phi_3:R\to D_3.
\]

If differently realized discriminators preserve compatible target distinctions, confidence increases that the relation is not an artifact of one narrow path.

Examples include:

```text
independent instruments
multiple laboratories
different measurement modalities
replicated datasets
cross-validation through action
```

Objectivity need not mean access without a discriminator.

It can mean constraint through multiple maintained discriminators whose failures and dependencies are sufficiently independent.

## 33. Composition and Irrecoverable Loss

Let:

\[
\Phi:R\to D
\]

and:

\[
\Psi:D\to E.
\]

If:

\[
\Phi(r_1)=\Phi(r_2),
\]

then:

\[
(\Psi\circ\Phi)(r_1)
=
(\Psi\circ\Phi)(r_2).
\]

A downstream operation cannot recover a distinction destroyed by every operative upstream path.

This applies to:

```text
sensor pipelines
recording systems
neural networks
communication channels
compilers
institutional procedures
scientific workflows
```

Recovery becomes possible only if another maintained path preserves information correlated with the lost distinction.

Thus the effective discriminator must include all operationally accessible side paths, records, memories, and external systems.

## 34. Refinement and Coarsening as Realized Change

Let:

\[
\Phi_1:R\to D_1
\]

and:

\[
\Phi_2:R\to D_2.
\]

If:

\[
\Phi_2(r_1)=\Phi_2(r_2)
\Longrightarrow
\Phi_1(r_1)=\Phi_1(r_2),
\]

then:

\[
\sim_{\Phi_2}\subseteq\sim_{\Phi_1}.
\]

The second mapping refines the first.

Realized changes that can produce refinement include:

```text
additional sensors
higher resolution
longer memory
better calibration
reduced interference
new external records
learned discrimination
new output paths
```

Coarsening may follow from:

```text
damage
noise
forgetting
compression
reduced energy
shortened horizon
removed sensors
lost access
```

The mathematical order on partitions remains unchanged.

The framework identifies the realized changes that move an organization within that order.

## 35. Learning Reorganizes the Generator

Let a learned discriminator be represented by:

\[
\Phi:R\to D.

Learning may produce:

\[
L(\Phi,\Delta)=\Phi'.
\]

The induced relation changes:

\[
\sim_{\Phi}
\longmapsto
\sim_{\Phi'}.
\]

Learning can:

```text
split a previous class
merge previous classes
stabilize a distinction
redirect attention
extend a temporal horizon
change which differences affect action
```

The learning process is itself realized and bounded.

It does not select distinctions from nowhere.

It reorganizes existing paths under the influence of encountered effects, maintained state, error, reinforcement, instruction, or social coordination.

## 36. Concepts Are Maintained Equivalence-Producing Organizations

A concept need not begin as an abstract class presented to an organization.

A system may first react similarly across different occasions.

Those stable similarities of operation generate an equivalence relation relative to the system and its continuation.

A later symbol may index the maintained organization.

The same word can participate in different mappings under different conditions.

For example, `dog` may support different operative classes in:

```text
road safety
veterinary diagnosis
genetics
legal ownership
image labeling
```

The concept is not arbitrary merely because it is indexed.

Its operative content is constrained by the realized alternatives, actions, histories, and couplings in which it is maintained.

## 37. Symbolic Reasoning Remains Realized

Formal symbols may be treated as stable identities within mathematics.

A realized symbolic system must nevertheless preserve some physical differences and ignore others.

Different inscriptions may count as the same letter.

Different bit patterns may encode the same value under a decoding rule.

Different derivations may be identified as reaching the same result.

Different expressions may be equivalent under a congruence.

These identities require realized encodings, recognition paths, conventions, and maintenance.

This does not reduce formal validity to hardware.

It separates:

```text
the validity of a formal relation
from
the realized conditions under which that relation becomes operative
```

## 38. Norms and Designs Can Reorganize Realization

A realized equivalence relation can later become explicit as a rule, standard, category, or design target.

The resulting formal object may then guide construction of new discriminators.

The causal order can therefore form a loop:

```text
realized interaction
    ↓
operative distinctions
    ↓
explicit classification or norm
    ↓
redesigned organization
    ↓
new realized mapping
```

A formal specification does not implement itself.

But once represented and maintained by an organization, it can guide the construction of mechanisms that realize its distinctions more reliably.

Thus formal relations can become causes within realized systems without becoming independent of realization.

## 39. What the Framework Does Not Claim

The framework does not claim:

```text
that mathematics must begin with physical realizations

that arbitrary formal equivalence relations are illegitimate

that every useful relation is uniquely determined by reality

that data is free from measurement and modeling choices

that all approximate similarities form equivalence relations

that every human desire follows automatically from bodily limitation

that technology removes boundedness

that formal infinity is invalid

that one realized implementation exhausts a theorem's meaning
```

It claims only that realized distinctions, identifications, measurements, concepts, computations, and actions require realized organization capable of producing and maintaining them.

When formal relations are used to explain those phenomena, the relations must remain answerable to that organization.

## 42. Central Principles

### Realizational-Primitive Principle

> When an equivalence relation describes realized operation, the realizational primitive is the bounded discriminator that generates the relation, not the relation taken alone.

### Answerability Principle

> A formal relation used to explain a realized phenomenon must be answerable to the organization, coupling, and maintenance through which it becomes operative.

### No-Free-Discrimination Principle

> No realized distinction or identification is available without realized structure capable of producing and maintaining it.

### Generated-Equivalence Principle

> Equality of operative outcomes under a realized mapping induces an equivalence relation.

### Boundedness Principle

> A bounded discriminator cannot preserve every possible distinction across every target, occasion, and horizon.

### Particularity Principle

> Boundedness forces some identification; the realized organization determines which identification occurs.

### Reliability Principle

> An operative distinction need not be perfect, but it must remain reliable enough under the conditions and horizon relevant to its continuation.

### Environmental-Coupling Principle

> The effective discriminator includes the environmental paths and interferences that affect which distinctions remain available.

### Data-Constraint Principle

> Data makes proposed relations answerable to realized interaction, while remaining indexed to the instruments and paths that produced it.

### Provenance Principle

> Formally identical structures can differ in explanatory relevance because their realized generators and histories differ.

### Extension Principle

> Tools reorganize boundedness by adding maintained paths through which new distinctions or actions become operative.

### Composite-Discriminator Principle

> External systems enlarge effective discrimination only when their states remain accessible through maintained coupling.

### Upstream-Loss Principle

> No downstream operation can recover a distinction erased by every available upstream path.

### Bounded-Lack Principle

> Experienced lack can emerge when a bounded organization preserves a contrast between its current capacities and another valued or imaginable continuation.

### No-Prior-Desire Principle

> A desire need not be assumed before realization; it may itself be generated through bounded interaction, memory, valuation, and comparison.

### Formal-Realizational Distinction

> Formal possibility does not entail realizational possibility, and realizational limitation does not invalidate formal mathematics.

### Potential-Unboundedness Principle

> A representation may permit arbitrarily extended finite operation without any realized execution becoming actually infinite.

### Explanatory-Sufficiency Principle

> The realized arrangement need only be specified at the level required to account for the operative distinction under investigation.

## 43. Central Statements

> Mathematics may begin with a given equivalence relation.

> A realizational explanation begins with a realized bounded discriminator.

> The discriminator does not receive a pre-partitioned target; it receives effects through coupling paths.

> Its organization produces an operative mapping.

> Equal operative outcomes induce equivalence classes.

> The realization is not an additional constraint on the mapping; it is what constrains and implements the mapping.

> No light distinction is operative without light-sensitive structure.

> No sound distinction is operative without vibration-sensitive structure.

> No temporal distinction is preserved without state maintained across the relevant horizon.

> No exception is caught without a path that distinguishes and redirects it.

> No action capacity exists without a mechanism capable of producing it.

> Perfect discrimination is not required; reliable-enough discrimination is itself indexed to bounded realization.

> Interference and finite resolution are consequences of realized coupling, not external objections to generated equivalence.

> Data matters because it constrains relations through realized interaction.

> Arbitrary character concatenations and recorded sequences may be formally alike while differing in realized provenance and explanatory relevance.

> Tools do not abolish boundedness; they reorganize the discriminator and alter its induced quotient.

> Many desires can be understood as later organizations emerging from encountered limitations, rather than as unexplained primitives.

> Python can support arbitrarily large finite integers in principle without any realized execution producing an actually infinite integer.

> Formal infinity and realized finite discrimination belong to different explanatory levels.

> A relation used to explain reality must be answerable to the realization that makes it operative.

## 44. Conclusion

The formal construction:

\[
R
+\sim
\longrightarrow
R/\!\sim
\]

remains valid and sufficient for mathematics.

When the relation is meant to describe a realized measurement, perception, computation, concept, procedure, or action, a different explanatory order becomes visible:

\[
\boxed{
\text{realized bounded discriminator}
\to
\text{operative mapping}
\to
\text{generated equivalence}
\to
\text{quotient structure}
}
\]

The mapping is not freely suspended between reality and mathematics.

It is the formal expression of what the realized organization can preserve, merge, maintain, and make available.

The realization itself supplies the relevant constraints.

Light-sensitive structure makes light differences operative.

Vibration-sensitive structure makes sound differences operative.

Memory makes temporally distributed differences jointly available.

Displays expose machine states to human discrimination.

Exception handlers separate continuations that would otherwise collapse into generic failure.

Microscopes, telescopes, thermal cameras, writing, databases, and other tools reorganize boundedness by introducing additional coupling and maintained state.

No such arrangement becomes unbounded.

Every realized discriminator remains finite in resolution, duration, energy, stability, and access. Environmental interference and internal variation prevent absolute perfection. The relevant standard is therefore not perfect identity of every microstate but reliable-enough production of the same operative outcome across the selected conditions and horizon.

This boundedness also clarifies the difference between formal and realizational possibility.

A formal system may describe infinite structures, arbitrary relations, and indefinitely extensible operations.

A realized computation can instantiate only finite states over finite occasions. Python may support integers of arbitrary finite size in its formal and implementation scheme, but no actual execution has produced an integer with infinitely many digits or completed infinitely many calculations.

The same distinction applies to equivalence.

A relation may be formally definable without being realized.

When it is offered as an explanation of a realized phenomenon, it must be answerable to the organization that implements it reliably enough.

This is also why data matters.

Data does not remove mediation, but it links proposed relations to realized traces. A sequence extracted through measurement and a sequence produced by arbitrary character concatenation may share formal properties while differing in provenance, constraint, and explanatory relevance.

Finally, boundedness need not merely limit an already desiring agent.

A realized organization may encounter effects or actions beyond its present capacities, preserve the contrast, experience the limitation as lack, and construct extensions of discrimination or action.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{
\text{realized boundedness}
\to
\text{encountered limitation}
\to
\text{operative lack}
\to
\text{attempted extension}
}
\]

Desire need not be placed outside the framework as an unexplained source of design. It can itself become a realized consequence of bounded interaction.

The central shift is therefore not from one mathematical definition to another.

It is from treating an equivalence relation as the first relevant fact to treating the realized bounded discriminator as the source from which operative equivalence arises.

\[
\boxed{
\text{Given }\sim
\quad\text{for formal mathematics;}
\qquad
\text{given a realized bounded discriminator}
\quad\text{for realizational explanation.}
}
\]
