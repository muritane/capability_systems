# From Lawful Evolution to Hierarchical Interaction Organization: Constraints, Realization, Scale, and Architecture-Relative Meaning

## Abstract

A system is not first a static thing that later happens to change.

What is observed is continuing evolution exhibiting stable regularities.

Bodies move, fields vary, atoms participate in quantum and electromagnetic dynamics, organisms metabolize, institutions process events, memories change, and technical systems transition among states. What is called `static` is ordinarily static only relative to a selected projection, timescale, tolerance, or observational resolution.

The foundational question is therefore not merely:

> Why do entities interact?

It is:

> How can persistent organization remain identifiable in a reality whose configurations continue to evolve according to stable regularities?

The starting point proposed here is **lawful continuation**:

\[
(X,\mathcal T),
\]

where \(X\) is a space of possible configurations and \(\mathcal T\) is a lawful transition structure specifying which continuations are physically admissible.

The essential empirical feature is not change alone. Arbitrary change would provide no stable basis for persistence, prediction, identity, or science. The essential feature is:

\[
\boxed{\text{continuing evolution under persistent constraints and regularities}.}
\]

Some regularities appear global and constitutive relative to everything presently realizable in our world. Others arise at particular scales. Some are evolved, self-maintaining, designed, institutional, intentional, or normative. These layers are not identical, but all realized higher-level organizations remain physically embedded within the more general transition structure of reality.

A **constraint** specifies or induces a restriction, selection, ordering, or dependency within a space of possible transitions.

An **interaction organizer** is a realized structure, process, or rule that embodies, maintains, exploits, or imposes such organization over a selected class of interactions.

The distinction is:

```text
lawful transition structure
→ admissible physical continuation

constraint
→ restriction or organization within a possibility space

interaction organizer
→ realized mechanism or architecture producing that organization
```

The framework is scale-independent because an interaction organizer may be:

```text
a field relation
a molecular geometry
a membrane
a metabolic loop
a nervous system
a compiler
a scheduler
a protocol
a court
a credential system
a social convention
```

These are not equivalent mechanisms. They occupy different organizational levels and have different explanatory requirements. What unifies them is that they structure which transformations occur, in what form, in what order, under which conditions, and with which retained consequences.

This produces a hierarchical account:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\text{lawful continuation}\\
&\rightarrow \text{globally admissible transition structure}\\
&\rightarrow \text{persistent regularities and invariants}\\
&\rightarrow \text{local and scale-relative constraints}\\
&\rightarrow \text{realized interaction organizers}\\
&\rightarrow \text{persistent organizations and boundaries}\\
&\rightarrow \text{interfaces, protocols, roles, agents, and institutions}\\
&\rightarrow \text{architecture-relative capabilities and meanings}.
\end{aligned}
}
\]

A predicate such as `talks`, `votes`, `owns`, `authenticates`, or `promises` is not meaningful merely because it is grammatically attachable to any noun. Its realization presupposes an interaction architecture capable of constituting the relevant act.

An electron can participate causally in the physical realization of speech. It does not, as a lone electron, instantiate the architecture required to be a speaker. The sentence “an electron talks to a human” is therefore not ordinarily a description of an unrealized but physically available capability. It applies a higher-level interaction predicate outside the architecture that gives that predicate determinate realization conditions.

The central proposal is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{Meaningful realized interaction is always relative to an organizing architecture nested within lawful reality.}
}
\]

## 1. Do Not Begin with Static Things

Ordinary language encourages a misleading sequence:

```text
first there is a thing
then the thing changes
then the thing interacts
```

But the thing is already identified through continuity across transformation.

A planet is not an exactly repeated microstate.

A body is not materially unchanged.

A living cell is not chemically static.

A memory is not physically identical from moment to moment.

An institution persists despite participant turnover.

A software service persists despite changing memory states, requests, processes, and hardware conditions.

The more accurate order is:

```text
continuing lawful evolution
→ recurrent or persistent organization
→ analytical individuation
→ entity attribution
→ identity judgment
```

A system is therefore not best understood as static substance plus accidental change.

It is a selected continuation pattern within ongoing lawful evolution.

---

## 2. Staticity Is Projection-Relative

The claim that nothing is absolutely static must be stated carefully.

It is not necessary to assert a complete metaphysical theorem that every conceivable aspect of reality changes at every instant.

The more defensible starting point is:

> No empirically accessible system is known as an absolutely changeless totality independent of scale, projection, horizon, and frame.

What appears static generally means that a selected observable remains within tolerance:

\[
d\!\left(P(x_t),P(x_{t+\Delta t})\right)<\varepsilon,
\]

where:

```text
P = selected projection
ε = accepted tolerance
Δt = selected interval
```

A building may be static relative to urban movement while its atoms, temperature, stresses, and electromagnetic relations continue to evolve.

A stationary quantum state may preserve observable probability distributions while its state acquires phase evolution.

An equilibrium may preserve macroscopic variables while microscopic processes continue.

A legal institution may preserve its name while its cases, personnel, records, obligations, and interpretations change.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{\text{staticity is ordinarily invariance under a selected projection, not cessation of all evolution}.}
\]

---

## 3. Lawful Continuation Rather Than Bare Change

Bare change is insufficient as a foundation.

If every configuration could be followed arbitrarily by every other configuration, there would be no stable basis for:

```text
prediction
identity
causation
memory
measurement
science
organization
```

The relevant primitive is not simply transformation.

It is **structured continuation**.

Let \(X\) be a configuration space.

Let:

\[
\mathcal T\subseteq X\times X
\]

be an admissible transition relation.

Then:

\[
x\rightarrow x'
\]

is physically admissible only when:

\[
(x,x')\in\mathcal T.
\]

For continuous-time descriptions, the same idea may be represented by permitted trajectories:

\[
\Gamma_{\mathrm{phys}}\subseteq \operatorname{Traj}(X).
\]

The world does not merely occupy states.

It continues along trajectories constrained by stable regularities.

---

## 4. Law Is Not Given Power by Description

A conservation equation does not cause conservation merely because it is written.

A mathematical representation does not grant reality its regularity.

The relation is the reverse:

```text
recurrent empirical regularity
→ compressed mathematical description
→ tested predictive structure
```

The equation acquires epistemic authority because it successfully describes regularities that are not produced by the description itself.

A conservation law points toward a stable feature across transformations:

\[
Q(x_t)=Q(x_{t+\Delta t})
\]

under the relevant conditions.

The important point is not that nothing changes.

It is that some quantity, relation, symmetry, or structural dependency remains invariant through change.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{\text{invariance is persistence within evolution, not the absence of evolution}.}
\]

---

## 5. Regularity Makes Persistence Possible

Persistent organization requires more than continuing change.

It requires that change have enough structure for some differences to recur, propagate, stabilize, or remain within a viability region.

If transition were wholly arbitrary, no organized distinction could be expected to continue.

Persistent organization therefore depends on constrained evolution.

Let \(P\) select an organization.

Let \(V_P\subseteq X\) be the states preserving that organization.

A continuation is viable over horizon \(H\) when:

\[
x_t\in V_P
\]

throughout the selected interval.

The existence of such trajectories depends on the structure of admissible evolution:

\[
\gamma\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{phys}}\cap \mathcal V_{P,H}.
\]

Persistent entities are therefore possible only because lawful evolution contains structured continuities.

## 6. Constraint Is Relative to a Possibility Space

A constraint is not meaningful without a prior or represented space of alternatives.

If only one transition is representable, there is nothing within that representation to distinguish as constrained.

Let \(\mathcal A(x)\) be a represented set of possible continuations from \(x\).

A constraint \(C\) may select:

\[
\mathcal A_C(x)\subseteq\mathcal A(x).
\]

But constraints need not operate only by subtraction.

They may also:

```text
order transitions
couple variables
set rates
define dependencies
stabilize attractors
allocate capacity
establish thresholds
require preconditions
produce recovery paths
```

Thus constraint should be understood broadly as **structured dependence within a possibility space**.

---

## 7. Constraint and Interaction Are Not Identical

Interaction is a dependency among transformations of distinguishable loci.

Constraint is a structure governing possible transformations.

An interaction may occur without a separately identifiable organizer.

A constraint may apply to a single modeled system without being described as an interaction between independent agents.

Suppose:

\[
x=(a,b,e).
\]

If:

\[
F_a(a,b,e)\neq F_a(a,b',e),
\]

then the evolution of \(a\) depends on \(b\).

That is interaction.

A constraint specifies something about the admissible or effective form of this dependency.

For example:

```text
only certain states may couple
the coupling has bounded strength
the effect propagates locally
the interaction preserves charge
the order of access must be serialized
authorization is required
```

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{\text{interaction is dependent transformation; constraint organizes its possibility or form}.}
\]

## 8. The Interaction Organizer

An interaction organizer is the realized structure that gives effective form to interaction constraints.

Let \(K\) be an organizer acting on state \(S\) and available transformations \(\mathcal I\):

\[
K(S,\mathcal I)
\rightarrow
\left(
\mathcal I^{\mathrm{admit}},
\mathcal I^{\mathrm{order}},
\mathcal I^{\mathrm{transform}},
\mathcal I^{\mathrm{retain}},
\mathcal I^{\mathrm{reject}},
\mathcal I^{\mathrm{release}},
\mathcal I^{\mathrm{repair}}
\right).
\]

Not every organizer performs every operation.

The general function is differential handling.

An interaction organizer may be realized by:

```text
geometry
dynamical coupling
chemical affinity
a membrane
a feedback loop
a scheduler
a lock
a type system
a communication protocol
a credential gate
a legal procedure
a social norm
```

It does not have to be conscious.

It does not have to be designed.

It does not have to represent its own rules.

It need only embody a stable organization of possible interactions.

## 9. Constraint Is Not Always an Organizer

The distinction between constraint and organizer should be retained.

A constraint may be represented abstractly:

\[
C:\mathcal A\mapsto\mathcal A_C.
\]

An organizer is a realized process or structure through which the constrained behavior occurs.

For example:

```text
Constraint:
    no simultaneous uncoordinated mutation of selected shared state

Organizer:
    mutex, ownership discipline, transaction system, actor model
```

```text
Constraint:
    only authorized parties may alter a record

Organizer:
    credential system, access-control service, audit process
```

```text
Constraint:
    selected molecules cross a boundary at different rates

Organizer:
    membrane structure, channel geometry, electrochemical gradients
```

Multiple organizers may realize similar constraints.

One organizer may embody several constraints.

## 10. Global and Local Transition Organization

Some regularities appear universal relative to all presently observed physical processes.

Others are local, contingent, historical, or designed.

This suggests a layered structure.

Let:

\[
\Gamma_0
\]

be the globally physically admissible trajectory space.

A local condition may select:

\[
\Gamma_1\subseteq\Gamma_0.
\]

A biological organization may further select:

\[
\Gamma_2\subseteq\Gamma_1.
\]

A technical architecture may select:

\[
\Gamma_3\subseteq\Gamma_2.
\]

An institutional rule may select:

\[
\Gamma_4\subseteq\Gamma_3.
\]

Schematically:

\[
\Gamma_0
\supseteq
\Gamma_1
\supseteq
\Gamma_2
\supseteq
\Gamma_3
\supseteq
\Gamma_4.
\]

This nesting is not always a simple literal set inclusion in practical models, because different levels use different state spaces and abstractions.

The more general principle is:

> Every realized higher-level interaction architecture must possess at least one physically admissible realization path within the lower-level transition structure.

## 11. Hierarchy Is Realizability, Not Simple Reduction

A legal procedure is physically realized.

It does not follow that its legal meaning can be adequately expressed as a list of particle trajectories.

A compiler is implemented through physical processes.

It does not follow that type correctness is best explained in the vocabulary of quantum fields.

A promise requires physical realization.

It does not follow that molecular description contains the normative conditions that make an utterance a promise.

The hierarchy therefore has two simultaneous properties:

### Physical embedding

Every realized higher-level organization depends on physically admissible processes.

### Explanatory autonomy

Higher-level distinctions may require concepts not recoverable as useful explanations merely by restating lower-level dynamics.

Thus:

\[
\boxed{\text{higher-level organization is physically constrained without being explanatorily exhausted by lower-level description}.}
\]

## 12. Constitutive Regularities

Constitutive regularities define or express the general transition structure within which all known realization occurs.

Examples may include:

```text
causal structure
locality conditions
conservation relations
quantum statistics
field dynamics
symmetry constraints
```

These regularities are not optional policies.

A lower-level or higher-level organizer cannot successfully realize a trajectory excluded by the constitutive structure of the world.

A constitution may authorize an institution to build a machine.

It cannot authorize a physically unrealizable machine into existence.

A programming language may define an operation.

The operation still requires a physically realizable implementation.

## 15. Normative Organizers

Normative organizers distinguish not merely what can happen but what counts as:

```text
permitted
prohibited
valid
invalid
authorized
obligatory
remediable
recognized
```

A legal right does not make an impossible physical transformation possible.

It changes the institutional status of physically possible transformations.

For example:

\[
\text{physically possible access}
+
\text{missing authorization}
\rightarrow
\text{institutionally prohibited access}.
\]

Normative organization introduces a distinction between:

\[
\operatorname{PhysicallyPossible}(x)
\]

and:

\[
\operatorname{InstitutionallyAdmissible}(x).
\]

The normative layer remains physically realized but cannot be derived from physical possibility alone.

## 16. Self-Reinforcing Organizers

Some organizers preserve or reproduce the conditions of their own continued operation.

Examples include:

```text
metabolic regulation
error-correcting memory
immune maintenance
institutional recordkeeping
legal precedent
protocol negotiation
cultural repetition
```

Let \(K_t\) be an organizer at time \(t\).

A self-reinforcing organizer contributes to trajectories satisfying:

\[
K_{t+\Delta t}\sim K_t
\]

under a selected equivalence relation.

This does not mean exact repetition.

It means that the organizer participates in maintaining the architecture that continues to organize later interactions.

Self-reinforcement is therefore a higher-order interaction:

\[
K \text{ organizes interactions that help preserve } K.
\]

```text
admission rules
```

A system that changes its own organizer is not outside constraint.

Its self-modification path must itself be admissible within:

```text
physical regularities
```

This yields recursive organization:

\[
K^{(2)}

organizes transformations of:

\[
K^{(1)},

Examples include:

```text
a constitution governing legislation
```

A boundary need not be an absolute wall.

It may be:

```text

```

A boundary is stabilized when the transition structure repeatedly distinguishes:

```text
inside from outside
retained from released
authorized from unauthorized
self-maintained from environmental
```

The boundary and the organization often co-determine one another.

Rather than a simple one-way derivation:

```text
persistence → boundary
```

the more accurate relation is:

\[
(P,B,H)\longleftrightarrow \text{identified persistence}.
\]

The projection \(P\), boundary \(B\), and horizon \(H\) jointly determine what counts as the continuing system.


At another:



Scale independence therefore means:

> The same organizational questions can be asked at multiple levels.

> All levels are the same kind of thing.

## 21. Capabilities Are Architecture-Relative

A capability is not simply an abstract verb attached to a thing.

It exists when there is a realizable path through an interaction architecture.

Let \(A\) be a selected architecture, \(\kappa\) a transformation, \(C\) a context, and \(H\) a horizon.

Then:

\[
\operatorname{Capable}(A,\kappa,C,H)
\iff
\exists p\;
\left[
p\in\Gamma_{\mathrm{phys}}
\land
\operatorname{Realizes}(p,\kappa,A,C,H)
\right].
\]

This separates:

```text
linguistic describability
logical consistency
physical possibility
architectural reachability
institutional admissibility
actual execution
```

A sentence may be grammatically valid while failing to identify any realizable capability path.

## 23. The Electron and the Human

Consider:

> An electron talks to a human.

The sentence is grammatically well formed.

But ordinary `talking` requires a large stack of organized interactions:

```text
stable matter
biological organization
nervous systems
memory
symbolic representation
language conventions
signal production
signal reception
semantic interpretation
speaker attribution
```

A single electron may participate causally in the physical implementation of some component of speech.

It does not, by itself, instantiate:

```text
a linguistic role
a semantic state
a speaker identity
a communication protocol
a capacity for commitment
```

Therefore the sentence is not normally false in the same way as:

> This human did not speak yesterday.

Instead, the subject fails to satisfy the architecture-relative type conditions of the predicate.

The statement resembles a type error:

```text
talks(electron, human)
```

where `talks` expects an occupant of a speaker-capable architecture.

## 24. Ill-Defined, False, and Fictional Must Be Distinguished

Not every non-realized statement fails in the same way.

### False but well-defined

> Alice talked to Bob yesterday.

The architecture exists, the predicate applies, but the event did not occur.

### Physically unrealized but conceptually specified

> A machine speaks fluently with a human.

The architecture may be specified and physically realizable even if no instance is currently available in the selected context.

### Fictionally stipulated

> The electron is a character that speaks.

A fictional architecture assigns agency and language to the electron-character.

### Architecture mismatch

> A lone physical electron promises to repay a loan.

The predicate presupposes organizational capacities absent from the selected subject.

This is not merely improbable.

It is ill-typed relative to the stated ontology.

## 25. Type Conditions for Interaction Predicates

An interaction predicate \(R(a,b)\) should specify:

```text
participant types
required capacities
interface conditions
protocol conditions
context
horizon
success criteria
```

Formally:

\[
R(a,b)
\]

is well-defined only if:

\[
a\in\operatorname{Dom}_1(R)
\quad\text{and}\quad
b\in\operatorname{Dom}_2(R),
\]

where the domains are architecture-relative, not merely linguistic categories.

For talking:

\[
\operatorname{Dom}_1(\operatorname{Talk})
\]

contains systems capable of occupying the speaker position in the relevant communication architecture.

This domain need not be restricted to humans.

A technical system may qualify if it realizes the relevant capacities.

A lone electron does not qualify merely because it participates in the system’s physical substrate.

A component may be necessary for a higher-level event without being the agent of that event.

Electrons participate in:

```text
speech production
neural activity
computation
memory storage
institutional communication
```

But this does not entail:

```text
the electron speaks
the electron remembers
the electron computes
the electron signs a contract
```

The inference fails because:

\[
\text{causal participation}
\neq
\text{occupation of the organized role}.
\]

Role attribution depends on the boundary and projection selected for the higher-level architecture.

This guards against both reductionist over-attribution and mystical agency assignment.

## 27. Lower Levels Enable Without Semantically Inheriting

The physical substrate enables language.

It does not follow that every physical component inherits linguistic predicates.

The hardware enables a software process.

It does not follow that each transistor independently runs the application.

The citizens realize an institution.

It does not follow that each citizen individually possesses every authority of the institution.

The relation is:

```text
lower-level realization
→ enables higher-level organization
```

not:

```text
lower-level component
→ inherits every higher-level predicate
```

This is a general anti-category-error principle.

## 28. Conservation Laws as Global Organizing Regularities

Conservation laws are especially important because they display persistence through transformation.

They do not say:

```text
nothing changes
```

They say:

```text
some quantity or relation remains invariant across admissible transformations
```

Schematically:

\[
Q(\gamma(t_1))=Q(\gamma(t_2))
\]

for trajectories and conditions under which the conservation statement applies.

In the present framework, conservation laws function as:

```text
global regularities of lawful continuation
```

They constrain the total architecture of physically realizable transformation.

They are not designed organizers.

They are not institutional rules.

They are not descriptions that compel reality.

They are successful representations of deep regularities exhibited by reality.

## 29. Symmetry and Invariance

A symmetry identifies transformations under which relevant structure remains unchanged.

Let \(G\) act on a state space \(X\).

A law or quantity is invariant when:

\[
Q(gx)=Q(x)
\]

for relevant \(g\in G\).

This provides a general pattern:

```text
transformation occurs
yet selected structure remains
```

Persistence is therefore not opposed to transformation.

It is frequently defined through transformation.

Identity, conservation, and organization all depend on specifying what remains invariant under which admissible changes.

> Persistent organization is identifiable through invariance, recurrence, or viability under lawful evolution.

## 31. Interaction Architecture as Constraint Composition

An architecture contains multiple organizers.

Let:

\[
\mathcal K=\{K_1,K_2,\ldots,K_n\}.
\]

Their effects may compose:

\[
K_{\mathrm{eff}}
=
K_n\circ K_{n-1}\circ\cdots\circ K_1.
\]

But composition may be:

```text
order-dependent
conflicting
redundant
hierarchical
partially overlapping
dynamically changing
```

In general:

\[
K_a\circ K_b\neq K_b\circ K_a.
\]

For example:

```text
physical transmission
→ network validation
→ application authorization
→ institutional approval
```

Changing the order may change the result.

Architecture is therefore not merely a set of constraints.

It is an organized composition of organizers.

## 32. Absolute, Strong, Weak, and Contingent Constraints

Constraints differ in scope and stability.

### Constitutive constraints

Apply to all known physical realization in the relevant domain.

### Structural constraints

Follow from the organization of a system, such as geometry or material composition.

### Viability constraints

Must be satisfied for a selected organization to persist.

### Protocol constraints

Define valid interaction sequences within a designed architecture.

### Institutional constraints

Determine recognized, authorized, or prohibited transitions.

### Preference constraints

Select among otherwise available continuations.

### Temporary constraints

Hold only under local, historical, or resource conditions.

These should not be collapsed into one category.

Their commonality is transition organization.

Their authority, origin, scope, and failure modes differ.

## 33. Violating a Rule and Failing to Realize a Transition

A person may violate a legal rule because the prohibited physical action remains possible.

A program may violate a protocol because malformed messages can still be emitted.

But a system cannot violate a constitutive physical constraint in the same sense.

If a proposed transition is excluded by the actual physical transition structure, it is not an available action waiting to be prohibited.

It is unrealizable.

Therefore:

```text
normative violation
≠
protocol invalidity
≠
physical impossibility
```

This distinction is essential.

Humans can invent:

```text
languages

software protocols
roles
rituals
markets
```

The symbolic specification may be extremely flexible.

But realization is constrained.

A game may stipulate teleportation as a fictional rule.

A physical implementation cannot produce literal teleportation merely because the game permits it.

Writing can describe an electron speaking.

The description does not establish a physical realization path.

The general principle is:

\[
\boxed{\text{symbolic possibility exceeds realized possibility}.}
\]

## 35. Description, Specification, and Realization

Three levels should be distinguished.

### Description

A representation says that something occurs.

### Specification

A representation defines conditions under which it would count as occurring.

### Realization

A physically admissible trajectory instantiates those conditions.

Thus:

\[
\operatorname{Described}(\kappa)
\not\Rightarrow
\operatorname{Specified}(\kappa)
\not\Rightarrow
\operatorname{Realizable}(\kappa)
\not\Rightarrow
\operatorname{Executed}(\kappa).
\]

A coherent framework must not move silently from words to realizability.

## 36. Architecture-Relative Truth Conditions

For many higher-level predicates, truth conditions depend on an architecture \(A\).

Write:

\[
\operatorname{Talk}_A(a,b,t).
\]

This means that within architecture \(A\):

```text
a occupies the speaker role
b occupies the receiver role
an admissible signal path occurs
the signal satisfies the relevant linguistic conditions
the event meets the architecture's success criterion
```

Without \(A\), the predicate may be underspecified.

The architecture may be:

```text
ordinary spoken language
sign language
text communication
machine-mediated dialogue
fictional narrative
```

The same surface verb can therefore refer to different realized interaction structures.

## 38. Purpose Is a Higher-Order Constraint

Purpose does not create physical possibility.

be the reachable trajectory space.

\[
\mathcal W^{\ast}_{A,C,H}
\subseteq
\mathcal W_{A,C,H}.

Thus:

```text
lawful possibility
precedes
architectural reachability
precedes
purposeful selection
```

Here `precedes` means analytical dependence, not necessarily temporal sequence.

## 40. Redesign Changes the Reachable Space

An unavailable interaction may become available after architectural transformation.

Let redesign produce:

\[

\[
\mathcal W_{K}
\neq
\mathcal W_{K'}.
\]

A system that cannot currently communicate linguistically might be embedded in a larger architecture that maps its physical states into symbols.

After such embedding, the larger system may participate in communication.

But the capability belongs to the composite architecture:

\[
A'=(\text{electron}+\text{detector}+\text{mapping}+\text{interface}+\text{interpreter}),
\]

not to the isolated electron.

This distinction preserves attribution accuracy.

## 43. A Hierarchical Realization Relation

Let \(A_{n}\) be an architecture at level \(n\).

Let \(A_{n-1}\) be a lower-level architecture.

Define:

\[
A_{n-1}\models A_n
\]

to mean that trajectories of \(A_{n-1}\) realize the organizational conditions specified by \(A_n\).

This does not imply identity:

\[
A_{n-1}\neq A_n.
\]

Nor does it imply semantic inheritance.

It means that the higher-level architecture has a lower-level realization.

A hierarchy may then be represented as:

\[
A_0\models A_1\models A_2\models\cdots\models A_n.
\]

For example:

```text
physical dynamics
⊨ electronic computation
⊨ software protocol
⊨ communication service
⊨ institutional transaction
```

## 44. Failure Modes Across Levels

Different levels fail differently.

### Physical failure

No admissible realization path exists.

### Structural failure

The material or geometric organizer no longer maintains the required interaction pattern.

### Capacity failure

Load exceeds retention, processing, release, or repair capacity.

### Protocol failure

The interaction sequence violates composition rules.

### Semantic failure

The signal does not satisfy the meaning conditions of the interaction.

### Institutional failure

The transition lacks recognition, authorization, or remedy.

### Attribution failure

A role or capability is assigned to the wrong organizational level.

The electron example is primarily an attribution and type failure.

## 46. Central Principles

### Lawful-Continuation Principle

> The foundational empirical structure is not bare change but continuing evolution restricted by stable regularities.

### Relative-Staticity Principle

> Staticity is invariance under a selected projection, frame, tolerance, and horizon rather than the demonstrated absence of all evolution.

### Invariance-Through-Change Principle

> Conservation and identity concern what remains stable through admissible transformation, not what never transforms.

### Description-Does-Not-Govern Principle

> Laws expressed mathematically derive authority from observed regularity and predictive success; descriptions do not compel reality to behave.

### Constraint-Relativity Principle

> A constraint is defined relative to a space of represented or physically admissible possibilities.

### Interaction-Constraint Distinction

> Interaction is dependent transformation; constraint organizes the possibility, form, order, or consequences of that transformation.

### Organizer-Realization Principle

> An interaction organizer is the realized mechanism, structure, or rule through which interaction constraints are embodied.

### Hierarchical-Embedding Principle

> Every realized higher-level organizer must possess a physically admissible implementation within lower-level transition structure.

### Explanatory-Autonomy Principle

> Physical realization does not eliminate the need for higher-level concepts such as protocol, meaning, authority, or obligation.

### Scale-Independence Principle

> Interaction organization can be analyzed at many scales through common structural questions, while mechanisms and meanings remain level-specific.

### Architecture-Relative-Predicate Principle

> Higher-level interaction predicates are well-defined only relative to architectures supplying participant types, interfaces, protocols, and success conditions.

### Participation-Role Distinction

> A component may causally participate in an event without occupying the higher-level role constituted by that event.

### Symbolic-Realization Principle

> A transition may be describable without being physically possible, architecturally reachable, or presently executable.

### Composite-Capability Principle

> Capabilities frequently belong to organized realization paths rather than isolated material components.

### Redesign Principle

> A capability created by embedding or redesign belongs to the new composite architecture, not retroactively to the original isolated system.

## 50. Conclusion

Reality is not known as a collection of absolutely static objects that occasionally undergo change.

What is observed is continuing evolution structured by persistent regularities.

The regularities matter because they make continuation non-arbitrary.

They permit invariants.

They permit recurrence.

They permit persistent distinctions.

They permit systems to remain identifiable while their realized states change.

Conservation laws are paradigmatic because they express persistence through transformation. Their power does not come from our inscriptions. Our inscriptions are valuable because they capture regularities that reality repeatedly exhibits.

Within this global lawful continuation, further constraints arise.

Some appear constitutive.

Some arise from geometry and material structure.

Some are evolved.

Some are self-reinforcing.

Some are designed.

Some are intentional.

Some are normative.

These constraints are not all identical, but they are hierarchically related through realization.

A lower-level transition structure makes higher-level organizers physically possible.

Higher-level organizers then create new effective distinctions:

```text
valid and invalid
authorized and unauthorized
speaker and listener
owner and non-owner
request and response
promise and non-promise
```

An interaction organizer is the scale-independent abstraction that captures this common form.

It is not simply another word for interaction.

Interaction is dependent transformation.

Constraint structures the possible form of that transformation.

The interaction organizer is the realized architecture that embodies, maintains, or imposes the constraint.

This distinction allows the same metamodel to analyze:

```text
fields
molecules
cells
organisms
software
institutions
language
rights
```

without pretending that they are materially or normatively identical.

It also clarifies why not every grammatically expressible interaction is realizable.

An electron can causally participate in speech.

A detector can translate electron states into symbols.

A larger composite architecture can use those symbols in communication.

But the lone electron does not thereby become a speaker.

The predicate `talks` belongs to an architecture containing the capacities, interfaces, roles, and semantic conditions that constitute talking.

Without that architecture, the attribution is not merely an unusual factual claim.

It is an organizational type error.

The deepest unifying statement is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\text{Reality continues lawfully.}\\
&\text{Regularities constrain its transitions.}\\
&\text{Persistent organizations embody further constraints.}\\
&\text{Interaction organizers realize those constraints at multiple scales.}\\
&\text{Capabilities and meanings arise only within such architectures.}
\end{aligned}
}
\]

The foundational question is no longer only:

> Which entity interacts with which other entity?

It becomes:

> Which trajectories are admissible; which regularities persist; which organization is being selected; which boundary and horizon make it identifiable; which constraints shape its interactions; which organizer realizes those constraints; how is that organizer embedded in lower-level lawful processes; and which higher-level predicates become well-defined only because that architecture exists?
