# Shared and Distinct: Composite Bounded Discrimination, Maintained Equivalence, and Multi-View Refinement

## Abstract

A bounded discriminator cannot preserve every distinction available in a realization.

Let:

\[
\Phi:R\to D
\]

map target realization states into operative discriminator states.

The mapping induces:

\[
r_1\sim_{\Phi}r_2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Phi(r_1)=\Phi(r_2).
\]

This explains how operative equivalence is generated by one bounded discriminator.

A further question arises when one discriminator is insufficient:

> Under which conditions can another bounded discriminator add distinctions rather than merely repeat, misalign, or fragment the first view?

The answer requires more than composition in the abstract.

Two views become jointly informative only when they are answerable to a sufficiently shared target, when their observations can be brought into correspondence, and when their couplings preserve at least some nonredundant differences.

For mappings:

\[
\Phi_1:R\to D_1,
\qquad
\Phi_2:R\to D_2,
\]

the composite mapping is:

\[
\Phi_C(r)
=
\bigl(\Phi_1(r),\Phi_2(r)\bigr).
\]

Its induced equivalence relation is:

\[
r_1\sim_C r_2
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Phi_1(r_1)=\Phi_1(r_2)
\text{ and }
\Phi_2(r_1)=\Phi_2(r_2).
\]

Therefore:

\[
\sim_C
=
\sim_{\Phi_1}\cap\sim_{\Phi_2}.
\]

The composite partition is the common refinement of the component partitions.

But strict refinement is not automatic.

If the second mapping is only a transformation of distinctions already available in the first, then it adds no new discrimination.

If the two mappings concern unrelated targets or cannot be aligned, then their product is juxtaposition rather than a richer view of one phenomenon.

If the mappings are identical but fail independently, repetition may increase reliability without increasing resolution.

The useful regime therefore requires both:

```text
shared enough
    → correspondence and joint attribution

distinct enough
    → nonredundant discrimination
```

This structure appears in binocular vision, multi-view reconstruction, multiple instruments, cross-laboratory replication, external memory, collaboration, compilers, institutional checks, education, safety systems, biological regulation, and other composite arrangements.

In each case, an assumption that a distinction is already shared or reliably maintained may fail.

The response is often to recruit or construct another realized organization that detects, preserves, checks, teaches, enforces, or restores the distinction.

A compiler replaces the assumption that a programmer will remember every typing constraint with a realized checking path.

Branch protection replaces the assumption that contributors will always remember a workflow with a repository state transition that refuses an inadmissible continuation.

A school does not make literacy an intrinsic property of every person. It is an institution intended to create, stabilize, test, and reproduce a capability that cannot simply be presumed.

A second camera does not abolish the limitations of the first. It adds a differently positioned projection whose overlap with the first can constrain a reconstruction.

The composite remains bounded.

Its gain is not the removal of equivalence but the reorganization of which distinctions are merged, which are preserved, which are jointly recoverable, and which failures remain tolerated.

The central claim is:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\text{No operative equivalence remains stable merely because it is stated.}\\
&\text{It is generated and maintained by realized discrimination.}\\
&\text{Composite discrimination adds information only through}\\
&\text{shared target constraint, correspondence, and nonredundant views.}
\end{aligned}
}
\]

## 1. From Generated Equivalence to Composite Discrimination

A single realized discriminator induces an equivalence relation through its operative mapping.

The basic dependency is:

```text
target realization
+ bounded discriminator
+ realized coupling
    ↓
operative mapping Phi
    ↓
induced equivalence ~Phi
```

This account explains why equivalence does not float arbitrarily when it is used to describe realized operation.

The mapping is constrained by:

```text
target organization
coupling path
discriminator architecture
resolution
noise
energy
memory
history
task
horizon
maintenance
```

But a local discriminator may merge a distinction required for a continuation.

The next question is then:

> Can another bounded discriminator preserve what the first one merges?

That question leads from generated equivalence to composite discrimination.

The composite problem is not solved by merely writing two outputs beside one another.

The views must be related strongly enough to constrain one phenomenon and different enough to add something that the other view lacks.

## 2. The General Form of `Works on My Machine`

The statement:

```text
works on my machine
```

usually suppresses an equivalence assumption:

\[
C_{\text{local}}
\approx
C_{\text{deployment}},
\]

where the contexts may include:

```text
operating system
library versions
compiler behavior
locale
permissions
environment variables
network access
timing
hardware
stored state
```

The code succeeds locally.

The speaker implicitly treats the distinctions between the two contexts as irrelevant.

Deployment failure reveals that at least one of those distinctions was operative.

The joke has a general form:

```text
my maintained context
    ↓
assumed equivalent to
    ↓
your maintained context
    ↓
reality reveals an untracked difference
```

The failure is not merely that someone held a false proposition.

The operative problem is that no realized arrangement was maintaining the required equivalence between contexts.

Containers, lock files, reproducible builds, environment specifications, automated tests, and continuous integration are attempts to move the burden from presumed contextual sameness into a maintained composite arrangement.

They do not make the contexts physically identical.

They preserve selected equivalences while controlling selected differences.

## 3. Presumed Knowledge and Realized Enforcement

A recurrent compression is:

> People know.

But this may conceal several different claims:

```text
someone once encountered the distinction
someone can state the distinction when asked
someone remembers it under normal conditions
someone notices when it becomes relevant
someone acts on it at the critical occasion
the organization prevents violation even if no one remembers
```

These are not equivalent.

Consider a protected branch.

The statement:

```text
contributors know not to push directly to main
```

attributes a distinction to people.

The repository rule:

```text
direct push
    → rejected transition
```

realizes the distinction in the workflow.

Likewise, a compiler check does not merely remind a programmer that a program is ill-typed.

It implements a discrimination between admissible and inadmissible continuations at a particular stage of the realization chain.

The relevant transition is:

```text
presumed distinction
    ↓
repeated or anticipated failure
    ↓
explicit discriminator
    ↓
maintained enforcement
```

The stronger organizational question is therefore not:

> Does someone know the rule?

It is:

> Which realized path preserves the distinction when memory, attention, interpretation, or intention fails?

## 4. Assumptions Are Compressed Context Claims

An assumption is often treated as a proposition admitted without proof.

In realized operation, an assumption frequently does more.

It compresses a context into a state such as:

```text
working
shared
understood
compatible
safe
available
known
maintained
```

For example:

```text
same language
same pronunciation
same alphabet
same listening conditions
same technical vocabulary
same interpretation of a term
```

may be compressed into:

```text
they will understand
```

The compression can be useful.

No conversation can explicitly reconstruct every linguistic and bodily dependency before each sentence.

But the compression becomes unsafe when the suppressed differences can alter the continuation.

Different languages, hearing conditions, accents, background knowledge, attention, and social conventions reveal that `understood` was not one context-free state.

The general question is:

> Which context distinctions have been merged into the assumption, and what restores them when the merger fails?

## 5. Schools as Maintained Capability Arrangements

Literacy cannot be assumed as an intrinsic property of every person.

A society that requires widespread reading and writing must realize paths through which the capability can be acquired, practiced, evaluated, and reproduced.

A school is one such arrangement.

Its operation may include:

```text
teachers
students
shared inscriptions
spoken instruction
curricula
repetition
correction
assessment
records
schedules
buildings
energy
institutional authority
```

The school does not guarantee identical literacy in every participant.

It is a bounded composite organization designed to make selected symbolic distinctions more widely and reliably available than they would be if left entirely to local accident.

The structural pattern is:

```text
capability cannot be presumed
    ↓
capability becomes socially required
    ↓
organization is created to generate and maintain it
    ↓
outputs are tested through further discriminators
```

This does not imply that every institution succeeds.

It means that institutions can be analyzed as realized arrangements for producing, stabilizing, checking, or enforcing distinctions that cannot safely remain assumptions.

## 6. Maintenance Is Not Added After Equivalence

A formal relation may be defined once:

\[
\sim\;\subseteq R\times R.
\]

An operative relation must continue to be produced across occasions.

The same target pair may be treated as equivalent at one time and distinct at another because of changes in:

```text
coupling
resolution
memory
training
energy
noise
context
standards
access
maintenance
```

Therefore the realizational dependency is not only:

\[
\mathfrak G
\longmapsto
\Phi_{\mathfrak G}
\longmapsto
\sim_{\Phi_{\mathfrak G}}.
\]

It also includes continued support:

\[
M_H(\mathfrak G)
\quad\Longrightarrow\quad
\text{the mapping remains operative over horizon }H.
\]

Without maintenance, the formal relation may remain describable while the operative equivalence disappears.

A language may remain documented while no current speaker can use it.

A file format may remain specified while no accessible decoder implements it.

A safety rule may remain written while no process detects or prevents its violation.

A road may remain present on a map while its realized surface no longer supports traffic.

The relevant principle is:

> A realized equivalence is only as operative as the arrangement that continues to generate, recognize, and use it.

## 7. One Bounded Discriminator Can Recruit Another

Suppose a discriminator:

\[
\Phi_1:R\to D_1
\]

merges states that a continuation requires it to separate.

Another discriminator may provide:

\[
\Phi_2:R\to D_2.
\]

The composite is:

\[
\Phi_C:R\to D_1\times D_2,
\]

with:

\[
\Phi_C(r)
=
\bigl(\Phi_1(r),\Phi_2(r)\bigr).
\]

The composite may distinguish target states merged by either component alone.

This structure occurs when:

```text
memory recruits writing
a person recruits another person
vision recruits a microscope
a program recruits a type checker
a laboratory recruits another instrument
a body recruits a regulatory subsystem
a group recruits an auditing organization
```

The language of recruitment does not imply intention in every case.

It denotes the addition or activation of a maintained path through which further distinctions become operative.

## 8. Composition Does Not Remove Boundedness

If:

\[
\Phi_1:R\to D_1
\]

and:

\[
\Phi_2:R\to D_2,
\]

then the composite state space is contained in:

\[
D_1\times D_2.
\]

If both components are bounded, their realized composite remains bounded.

Even when the formal product is large, the operative composite is constrained by:

```text
which joint states can actually occur
which states can be synchronized
which paths remain accessible
which histories are retained
which outputs can be compared
which resources maintain the coupling
```

The addition of another discriminator therefore does not create an unbounded observer.

It reorganizes boundedness.

The new composite may have:

```text
finer resolution
longer horizon
higher reliability
new failure tolerance
new action capacity
new blind spots
new coordination costs
```

Every gain is selective.

## 9. Shared Target Is Required for Joint Refinement

Two mappings can be written side by side without describing two views of one phenomenon.

Suppose:

\[
\Phi_1:R_1\to D_1,
\qquad
\Phi_2:R_2\to D_2.
\]

If no relation connects \(R_1\) and \(R_2\), then the pair:

\[
\bigl(\Phi_1(r_1),\Phi_2(r_2)\bigr)
\]

is merely a joint record of two potentially unrelated events.

To function as multiple views of one phenomenon, the observations must be attributable to a sufficiently shared target arrangement.

This does not require that the views expose identical target regions or use identical coordinates.

It requires a maintained basis for saying that their differences arise from related portions, states, histories, or consequences of one target organization.

The shared-target condition may be expressed through a target \(R\) and view mappings:

\[
\Phi_i:R\rightharpoonup D_i,
\]

where the partial arrow allows each view to access only part of the target.

The common target prevents arbitrary concatenation from being mistaken for composite discrimination.

## 10. Overlap Is Required for Correspondence

If two views have no operative overlap, there may be no basis for determining how their outputs align.

Let:

\[
\operatorname{dom}(\Phi_1),
\qquad
\operatorname{dom}(\Phi_2)
\]

be the target regions or states available to two partial views.

Their overlap is:

\[
O_{12}
=
\operatorname{dom}(\Phi_1)
\cap
\operatorname{dom}(\Phi_2).
\]

When:

\[
O_{12}=\varnothing,
\]

the views may still be related through an external model, known transformation, causal chain, or shared reference.

But the views themselves provide no direct common target states through which correspondence can be established.

Overlap supplies candidate invariants:

```text
same feature under different projection
same event recorded by different sensors
same specimen under different measurement modalities
same code executed in different environments
same claim examined by different laboratories
same student capability examined through different tasks
```

Correspondence is itself a discrimination.

The composite must distinguish:

```text
these two outputs concern the same target feature
versus
these outputs merely resemble one another
```

Without a realized correspondence path, the common target may be asserted rather than demonstrated.

---

## 11. Distinct Views Are Required for New Discrimination

Shared target and overlap are not sufficient for a gain in resolution.

Suppose:

\[
\Phi_2=f\circ\Phi_1
\]

for some transformation:

\[
f:D_1\to D_2.
\]

Then every distinction made by \(\Phi_2\) was already available through \(\Phi_1\).

The second view may recode, summarize, display, or transmit the first.

But it cannot separate target states that \(\Phi_1\) already merged.

If:

\[
\Phi_1(r)=\Phi_1(r'),
\]

then:

\[
\Phi_2(r)
=
f(\Phi_1(r))
=
f(\Phi_1(r'))
=
\Phi_2(r').
\]

No new target distinction appears.

A second camera at the same position with the same optics and noiseless operation induces the same geometric projection.

A copied file does not add semantic distinctions merely because it occupies another disk region.

A second person repeating the same assumption does not create an independent check.

For strict refinement, at least one component must preserve a target distinction merged by another.

## 12. The Common-Refinement Construction

Let:

\[
\Phi_1:R\to D_1,
\qquad
\Phi_2:R\to D_2.
\]

Each mapping induces an equivalence relation:

\[
r\sim_1 r'
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Phi_1(r)=\Phi_1(r'),
\]

\[
r\sim_2 r'
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Phi_2(r)=\Phi_2(r').
\]

Define the composite:

\[
\Phi_C(r)
=
\bigl(\Phi_1(r),\Phi_2(r)\bigr).
\]

Then:

\[
\begin{aligned}
r\sim_C r'
&\Longleftrightarrow
\Phi_C(r)=\Phi_C(r')\\
&\Longleftrightarrow
\Phi_1(r)=\Phi_1(r')
\text{ and }
\Phi_2(r)=\Phi_2(r')\\
&\Longleftrightarrow
r\sim_1 r'
\text{ and }
r\sim_2 r'.
\end{aligned}
\]

Therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\sim_C
=
\sim_1\cap\sim_2
}
\]

The composite equivalence is the intersection of the component equivalence relations.

Its classes are the nonempty intersections of component classes:

\[
[r]_C
=
[r]_1\cap[r]_2.
\]

The composite cannot be coarser than either component:

\[
\sim_C\subseteq\sim_1,
\qquad
\sim_C\subseteq\sim_2.
\]

But it may equal one or both.

---

## 13. Strict Refinement

The composite strictly refines the first mapping when:

\[
\sim_C
\subsetneq
\sim_1.
\]

This occurs exactly when there exist target states \(r,r'\in R\) such that:

\[
\Phi_1(r)=\Phi_1(r')
\]

but:

\[
\Phi_2(r)\neq\Phi_2(r').
\]

The second discriminator then preserves a distinction lost by the first.

Likewise, the composite strictly refines both components when each component preserves at least one distinction lost by the other.

This is the regime of nonredundant complementarity.

It can be represented as:

```text
Phi_1 separates some pairs merged by Phi_2
+
Phi_2 separates some pairs merged by Phi_1
    ↓
composite strictly refines both
```

The two component partitions are then incomparable under refinement, while their intersection is finer than either.

## 14. Redundancy, Reliability, and Resolution Must Be Separated

A repeated view may add value even when it does not refine the induced partition.

Suppose two discriminators implement approximately the same mapping but have partially independent failure modes.

They may induce the same intended classes while producing different errors on different occasions.

Combining them can increase confidence or fault tolerance.

This is not the same as increasing resolution.

At least four gains should be distinguished.

### Partition gain

Previously merged target states become distinguishable.

### Reliability gain

The same distinction is produced more consistently across noise, damage, or occasions.

### Horizon gain

A distinction remains accessible for longer through memory, records, or repeated measurement.

### Action gain

A distinction that was already available can now alter a continuation through a new control or enforcement path.

Two identical cameras may add little geometric resolution but support failure detection.

Two laboratories using nearly identical procedures may add replication evidence.

A written rule may add little if everyone already remembers it, but an interlock can add action gain by preventing violation.

A useful composite analysis must therefore specify which kind of gain is claimed.

## 15. Distinct Position Is One Source of Nonredundancy

In multi-view geometry, different positions generate different projections of a shared scene.

The difference in position matters because each view merges depth and spatial relations differently.

A second view can therefore separate some three-dimensional possibilities that remain equivalent under the first projection.

The relevant structure is not merely:

```text
two cameras
```

It is:

```text
shared scene
+ overlapping visible structure
+ different projection centers
+ correspondence between image features
+ sufficient calibration or recoverable geometry
```

If the positions are identical and the projection mechanisms are identical, the geometric partitions may be the same.

If the positions are too different and no overlapping structure remains recognizable, correspondence may fail.

The useful regime requires a constrained difference:

```text
enough shared structure to align
+
enough positional difference to constrain ambiguity
```

This is one concrete instance of a more general composite-discrimination condition.

## 16. Correspondence Is Not Free

The notation:

\[
\Phi_1:R\to D_1,
\qquad
\Phi_2:R\to D_2
\]

already assumes that both mappings share the same target parameterization.

In realized systems, that parameterization may need to be constructed.

One sensor may use image coordinates.

Another may use time-of-flight measurements.

A third may use symbolic labels.

A human report may use language and memory.

To combine them, an alignment arrangement must preserve relations such as:

```text
same target locus
same event
same time interval
same object identity
same experimental condition
same unit
same coordinate frame
```

Let:

\[
A_{12}:D_1\times D_2\rightharpoonup C_{12}
\]

represent a correspondence process.

Its output may distinguish:

```text
aligned
not aligned
ambiguous
insufficient overlap
conflicting identity
```

The correspondence process is itself bounded.

It can create false matches, miss true matches, or rely on assumptions that later fail.

Composite discrimination therefore contains at least two levels:

```text
discriminate target variation within each view
+
discriminate which outputs correspond across views
```

## 17. Partial Views and Patchwise Composition

Realized views often access only part of a target.

Let:

\[
\Phi_i:R\rightharpoonup D_i
\]

be partial mappings.

For each target state \(r\), define the available index set:

\[
I(r)
=
\{i:\Phi_i(r)\text{ is available}\}.
\]

A patchwise composite may be written:

\[
\Phi_C(r)
=
\bigl(\Phi_i(r)\bigr)_{i\in I(r)}.
\]

But two target states with different available index sets cannot be compared merely by equality of tuples.

The composite must also represent missingness, occlusion, inaccessibility, or failure.

The state:

```text
not observed
```

must not silently become equivalent to:

```text
observed and absent
```

This is a recurring problem across:

```text
camera occlusion
medical testing
missing database fields
failed sensors
unanswered survey questions
unavailable witnesses
unreadable records
```

A well-formed composite discriminator preserves the distinction between target absence and path failure when that distinction matters.

## 18. Multi-View Reconstruction as Constraint Intersection

A single view often leaves many target realizations compatible with its output.

For an output \(d_i\in D_i\), define the compatible target set:

\[
C_i(d_i)
=
\{r\in R:\Phi_i(r)=d_i\}.
\]

This is the fiber:

\[
C_i(d_i)=\Phi_i^{-1}(d_i).
\]

Given two outputs:

\[
d_1=\Phi_1(r),
\qquad
d_2=\Phi_2(r),
\]

the targets compatible with both are:

\[
C_{12}(d_1,d_2)
=
C_1(d_1)\cap C_2(d_2).
\]

Reconstruction becomes more constrained when this intersection is smaller than either fiber alone.

In the ideal unique case:

\[
C_1(d_1)\cap C_2(d_2)=\{r\}.
\]

Realized systems rarely achieve perfect uniqueness across every target and condition.

But the intersection formulation shows why shared and distinct views matter.

Shared target makes the intersection meaningful.

Distinct mappings make the intersection potentially smaller.

Correspondence determines whether the outputs may legitimately be intersected as constraints on one target state.

## 19. Different Views Can Also Conflict

A composite does not automatically produce agreement.

Suppose two maintained mappings yield outputs that cannot be jointly satisfied under the current target model.

Then:

\[
C_1(d_1)\cap C_2(d_2)=\varnothing.
\]

The empty intersection may indicate:

```text
measurement error
misalignment
target change between occasions
incorrect calibration
incorrect shared-target attribution
incompatible models
path interference
fraud or corrupted records
```

Conflict is itself informative only if the composite preserves where the inconsistency arises.

A system that merely averages incompatible outputs may destroy the distinction between:

```text
uncertainty within one model
and
inconsistency between realized paths
```

The composite should therefore be able to represent disagreement rather than forcing every set of views into one apparently coherent state.

## 20. Cross-Realization Objectivity

Objectivity need not require a view from nowhere.

It can be analyzed as constraint through multiple realized paths.

Suppose:

\[
\Phi_1:R\to D_1,
\qquad
\Phi_2:R\to D_2,
\qquad
\Phi_3:R\to D_3.
\]

Confidence increases when:

```text
the views are attributable to a shared target
+
the correspondence is maintained
+
the relevant failures are not all shared
+
the mappings preserve compatible distinctions
```

The independence condition is never absolute.

Two laboratories may use the same instrument design.

Two witnesses may share a rumor.

Two models may inherit the same dataset bias.

Two sensors may fail under the same environmental condition.

A composite analysis should therefore identify which dependencies are shared and which are distinct.

Objectivity becomes less a count of agreeing outputs and more an audit of the realization paths that produced them.

## 21. Composition Across Biological Scales

The same formal pattern may occur at several biological scales, but the domain-specific mechanisms must remain explicit.

A cell may contain pathways that detect internal damage and initiate self-destruction.

Other cells or extracellular processes may detect states that the local pathway fails to handle.

An organ may possess local regulation while another organ or systemic process partially compensates for loss.

A nervous system may recruit external tools, other people, records, or institutions when local memory and discrimination are insufficient.

The common form is:

```text
local organization merges or fails to control a consequential state
    ↓
another path preserves or detects a relevant difference
    ↓
the composite supports a different continuation
```

The framework should not infer that every biological replacement is exact.

Compensation may be:

```text
partial
temporary
costly
asymmetric
condition-dependent
```

Nor should every biological interaction be described as discrimination without identifying the operative state differences and continuations involved.

Scale independence is a structural hypothesis, not a license to erase domain-specific organization.

## 22. Other Brains as Additional Partitions

One person cannot preserve every distinction relevant to every task.

Another person may possess:

```text
different sensory access
different training
different memory
different language
different social position
different error history
different incentives
```

The second person therefore need not induce the same partition of a target.

Collaboration can refine the composite only when the views can be aligned around a shared question, event, object, or task.

Without shared attribution, the participants may simply discuss different phenomena.

Without distinct experience or organization, the group may reproduce one local partition many times.

Without a maintained communication path, distinctions available to one person never become operative in the composite.

Without procedures for representing disagreement, the group may coarsen its outputs into premature consensus.

A group is therefore not automatically a better discriminator than an individual.

Its advantage depends on the organization of overlap, difference, correspondence, communication, memory, and evaluation.

## 23. Groups Can Add Partitions of Reality

Different groups may organize different distinctions around the same target.

For example, a building can be partitioned through:

```text
structural safety
fire safety
accessibility
energy use
legal ownership
maintenance cost
occupant experience
```

These are not necessarily competing descriptions.

They are different mappings from a shared realized organization into different operative state spaces.

A composite analysis can preserve them jointly when their target attributions and boundary assumptions remain explicit.

Conflict arises when one partition is silently treated as exhaustive.

For example:

```text
financially equivalent
```

need not imply:

```text
structurally equivalent
```

or:

```text
accessible to the same occupants.
```

The framework therefore permits multiple legitimate partitions while requiring each to remain answerable to its realized discriminator, task, and maintained context.

## 24. Engineering: Post-Factum Repair or Prospective Realization?

Engineering often develops through failure.

A system is built.

A distinction was assumed.

Reality exposes the missing distinction.

A detector, rule, barrier, or recovery path is added.

This can look like a sequence of workarounds.

But the same structure can be used prospectively.

A design can ask before failure:

```text
Which distinctions must remain operative?
Where are they currently maintained?
Which components share a failure mode?
Which assumptions compress incompatible contexts?
What detects loss before damage propagates?
Which alternative path preserves the distinction?
```

Compilers, static analyzers, interlocks, type systems, formal verification, simulations, hazard analyses, tests, redundancy, and monitoring can be understood as attempts to construct discriminators before an unhandled failure occurs.

Engineering is post-factum when it waits for reality to reveal the missing partition.

It becomes systematic when it explicitly models the distinctions required for safe or adequate continuation and locates the realized organizations that maintain them.

## 25. The Assumption–Failure–Maintenance Cycle

A recurrent historical cycle is:

```text
maintained background becomes reliable
    ↓
higher layer compresses it into an assumption
    ↓
the maintenance becomes invisible
    ↓
context changes or maintenance fails
    ↓
a suppressed distinction becomes consequential
    ↓
new checks, tools, roles, or institutions are added
    ↓
a new composite becomes reliable
    ↓
its operation is compressed into another assumption
```

Examples include:

```text
manual deployment
    → reproducible build systems

verbal safety instruction
    → interlocks and alarms

informal contribution norms
    → protected branches and required review

local memory
    → writing and databases

presumed literacy
    → educational institutions

single measurement
    → calibration, replication, and multi-modal measurement
```

The cycle explains why infrastructure often becomes visible only through failure.

The higher layer did not create its own stability.

It relied on a maintained composite whose work had been compressed into the state:

```text
available
```

or:

```text
working.
```

## 26. Formalism and Implementation

Formalism and implementation differ because they answer different questions.

A formalism may state:

```text
assume P
let ~ be an equivalence relation
let Phi be a function
let the channel be given
let the data be sampled
```

This can be entirely legitimate for deduction.

A realizational explanation asks:

```text
What makes P hold?
Which organization implements ~?
Which coupling produces Phi?
Which maintained path constitutes the channel?
Which instrument and procedure produce the sample?
```

The discrepancy becomes problematic when a formal assumption is transferred into a claim about realized capability without identifying the realization that supports it.

The problem is not abstraction itself.

The problem is an unnoticed change in explanatory level.

The formal object remains valid.

The realizational claim remains underdetermined until the relevant generator, correspondence, and maintenance conditions are supplied.

## 27. No Floating Equivalence Principle

The phrase `floating equivalence` can be made precise.

A formal equivalence relation floats relative to a realizational explanation when the explanation invokes the relation but does not identify:

```text
the target states over which it operates
the discriminator that induces or enforces it
the coupling through which target differences become available
the conditions under which the classes remain stable
the continuation for which the equivalence matters
the evidence that the equivalence is operative
```

This does not make the formal relation arbitrary as mathematics.

It makes its application to the realized phenomenon insufficiently grounded.

The corresponding principle is:

> No operative equivalence is stabilized merely by being stated. Its classes remain available only through realized mappings, correspondence paths, and maintenance conditions that continue to produce and use the relevant identifications.

## 28. Composite Answerability

A local equivalence relation is answerable to one discriminator arrangement.

A composite relation has additional burdens.

It must be answerable to:

```text
each component mapping
the shared target attribution
the overlap conditions
the correspondence mechanism
the synchronization or temporal relation
the joint maintenance path
the procedure for handling disagreement
```

The composite relation:

\[
\sim_C
=
\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\sim_i
\]

is mathematically well-defined when all relations share a domain.

In a realized system, the shared domain is itself an achievement.

It may depend on:

```text
registration
identity tracking
common units
shared clocks
reference standards
sample provenance
record linkage
translation
institutional recognition
```

The mathematics of intersection does not supply these bridges.

The bridges make the intersection operative.

## 29. A Minimal Composite Arrangement

A composite realized discriminator may be represented as:

\[
\mathfrak C
=
(R,\{A_i\},\{P_i\},O,H,\{D_i\},\{\Phi_i\},K,S,M,E),
\]

where:

```text
R       = shared or partially shared target realization
A_i     = component discriminator organizations
P_i     = component propagation and transformation paths
O       = interaction occasions
H       = selected temporal horizon
D_i     = operative state spaces
Phi_i   = component discrimination mappings
K       = correspondence and alignment organization
S       = synchronization, ordering, or shared-reference structure
M       = maintenance conditions
E       = task, continuation, or evaluation relation
```

For a fully shared target domain, define:

\[
\Phi_C(r)
=
\bigl(\Phi_1(r),\ldots,\Phi_n(r)\bigr).
\]

The induced equivalence is:

\[
r\sim_{\mathfrak C}r'
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\forall i,
\Phi_i(r)=\Phi_i(r').
\]

Thus:

\[
\sim_{\mathfrak C}
=
\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\sim_{\Phi_i}.
\]

The composite is realizationally adequate only when the alignments and joint comparisons required by this formula are themselves supported by \(K\), \(S\), and \(M\).

## 30. Failure Modes of Composite Discrimination

### No shared target

The outputs concern unrelated realizations.

Possible failure:

```text
juxtaposition is mistaken for joint evidence
```

### No overlap or bridge

The views may concern one larger target, but no operative correspondence can be established.

Possible failure:

```text
different fragments are asserted to be the same phenomenon without alignment
```

### Redundant mapping

One view is a transformation or repetition of another.

Possible failure:

```text
more outputs are mistaken for more target information
```

### Shared failure path

Multiple components inherit the same hidden dependency.

Possible failure:

```text
agreement is mistaken for independent confirmation
```

### Misregistration

Outputs are attributed to the wrong target loci, occasions, or identities.

Possible failure:

```text
the composite refines an incorrect correspondence
```

### Forced consensus

Disagreement is averaged or suppressed.

Possible failure:

```text
inconsistency is compressed into false certainty
```

### Coordination overload

The cost of aligning and maintaining views exceeds the gain.

Possible failure:

```text
more components reduce effective discrimination
```

### Lost accessibility

An external discriminator exists but its state cannot reach the continuation.

Possible failure:

```text
nominal composite capacity is credited to an inaccessible component
```

### Horizon mismatch

The views concern target states at incompatible times.

Possible failure:

```text
target change is mistaken for observer difference
```

## 32. Central Principles

### Operative-Maintenance Principle

> A distinction remains operative only while some realized organization continues to generate, preserve, recognize, and use it across the required occasions and horizon.

### Assumption-Compression Principle

> An operative assumption commonly compresses a maintained context into a state such as `shared`, `working`, `known`, or `safe`, while suppressing distinctions that may reappear under changed conditions.

### Presumption–Enforcement Distinction

> Attributing knowledge or intention to participants is not equivalent to realizing a path that detects or prevents an inadmissible continuation.

### Externalized-Discrimination Principle

> Tools, rules, checks, records, institutions, and other agents enlarge a local discriminator only when their states remain accessible through maintained coupling.

### Composite-Boundedness Principle

> A composition of bounded realized discriminators remains bounded, even when its operative state space, horizon, reliability, or action capacity exceeds that of any component.

### Shared-Target Principle

> Multiple outputs constitute views of one phenomenon only when a maintained relation attributes them to a sufficiently shared target realization.

### Overlap Principle

> Direct cross-view correspondence requires shared target structure, or an alternative realized bridge capable of relating nonoverlapping observations.

### Nonredundancy Principle

> A second view adds target discrimination only when it preserves at least one distinction merged by the first.

### Common-Refinement Principle

> For mappings over a shared domain, the composite mapping induces the intersection of the component equivalence relations.

### Strict-Gain Principle

> Composite discrimination strictly refines a component exactly when another component separates at least one target pair merged by that component.

### Reliability–Resolution Distinction

> Repeated views may improve confidence or fault tolerance without refining the induced target partition.

### Correspondence-Is-Realized Principle

> The attribution of outputs to the same target locus, event, object, or interval is itself an operative discrimination requiring realized support.

### Conflict-Preservation Principle

> A composite discriminator should preserve incompatible outputs as disagreement when no target state satisfies all maintained constraints.

### Cross-Realization Constraint Principle

> Agreement is stronger when compatible distinctions arise through differently organized paths whose relevant failures are not all shared.

### Scale-Transfer Principle

> The formal pattern of bounded discrimination, external support, and composite refinement may recur across biological, cognitive, technical, and institutional scales, while the realized mechanisms remain domain-specific.

### Prospective-Engineering Principle

> Engineering becomes systematic when required distinctions, their maintaining organizations, and their failure paths are identified before unhandled loss occurs.

### No-Floating-Equivalence Principle

> A formal relation may be freely specified, but an equivalence used to explain realized operation must remain answerable to the mappings, correspondence paths, and maintenance conditions through which it becomes operative.

## 35. Conclusion

A bounded discriminator induces equivalence by mapping multiple target realizations into operative states.

This explains how one realized partition arises.

But bounded organizations often encounter distinctions that their local mappings fail to preserve.

The response may be to recruit another sensor, another cell, another organ, another person, another record, another instrument, another program analysis, another institution, or another realized path.

The result is not an unbounded discriminator.

It is another bounded composite.

The composite becomes informative only under specific conditions.

Its components must be attributable to a sufficiently shared target.

Their outputs must be aligned through overlap, reference structure, identity tracking, synchronization, or another realized correspondence path.

Their mappings must differ in a way that preserves nonredundant target distinctions.

When these conditions hold, the composite mapping:

\[
\Phi_C(r)
=
\bigl(\Phi_1(r),\ldots,\Phi_n(r)\bigr)
\]

induces:

\[
\sim_C
=
\bigcap_{i=1}^{n}\sim_{\Phi_i}.
\]

The composite equivalence is the common refinement of the component equivalences.

Strict refinement occurs when one view separates a target pair merged by another.

Repetition without such separation may still increase reliability, but it does not add resolution.

Difference without shared target does not produce a richer view of one phenomenon.

Shared target without difference produces redundancy.

The generative structure is therefore:

\[
\boxed{
\begin{aligned}
&\text{shared target}\\
&+\;\text{maintained correspondence}\\
&+\;\text{nonredundant bounded views}\\
&\longrightarrow\;\text{composite refinement}
\end{aligned}
}
\]

This structure clarifies why assumed contexts repeatedly fail.

`Works on my machine` assumes an equivalence between environments that no arrangement has fully maintained.

`People know` assumes that a distinction will remain available at the critical occasion.

`They will understand` assumes shared language, segmentation, identity, pronunciation, attention, and context.

`The rule exists` assumes that a formal statement is enough to alter realized continuation.

Reality repeatedly exposes where these compressed equivalences fail.

Engineering and institutions then add realized discriminators:

```text
compiler checks
protected branches
interlocks
alarms
records
schools
standards
independent measurements
review procedures
```

These can look like isolated workarounds when examined locally.

Under the present framework, they share a common role.

They relocate distinctions from presumed cognition or background context into maintained composite organization.

The strongest form of the framework is therefore not merely:

> Equivalence is generated by a discriminator.

It is:

> Operative equivalence is generated, aligned, maintained, tested, and revised by bounded realized discriminators whose composition is informative only when shared target constraint and nonredundant difference are both preserved.

The final accounting remains bounded.

Every composite leaves distinctions unresolved.

Every correspondence can fail.

Every institution depends on maintenance.

Every abstraction suppresses context.

The purpose is not to eliminate abstraction.

It is to make the realization that carries the abstraction recoverable when the continuation depends on it.

\[
\boxed{
\text{No shared distinction without shared realization support;}
\qquad
\text{no new composite distinction without realized difference.}
}
\]
