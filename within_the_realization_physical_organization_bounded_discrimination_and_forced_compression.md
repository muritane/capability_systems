# Within the Realization: Physical Organization, Availability, Bounded Discrimination, and Forced Compression

## Abstract

A modeler is often described as though it stood outside the reality being modeled.

Reality appears on one side.

The observer, representation, or formalism appears on the other.

That picture introduces a separation before demonstrating that any such separation is required.

A different starting point is available:

> The discriminator, its representations, its media, and its acts of discrimination are themselves realized organizations within the same reality in which their targets occur.

Thoughts are realized through organized bodily activity.

Equations are realized as marks, sounds, electromagnetic states, memory configurations, or other physically maintained differences.

Software source, compiled binaries, processor states, display pixels, photons, retinal responses, and later interpretation form connected realization chains rather than transitions between a physical realm and a nonphysical one.

This does not eliminate identity.

Organizations sharing a substrate can remain distinct through boundaries, couplings, persistence conditions, and re-identification criteria. A person, monitor, cable, processor, token sequence, and institution may all be physically realized without becoming one undifferentiated object.

The central limitation arises elsewhere.

A bounded discriminator can realize only some range of mutually distinguishable states. If the realization space presented to it contains more distinguishable states than the discriminator can preserve, then multiple realization states necessarily produce the same operative discriminator state.

Let:

\[
\Phi:R\to D
\]

map target realizations in \(R\) to discriminator states in \(D\).

If:

\[
|R|>|D|,
\]

then \(\Phi\) cannot be injective. At least two distinct realizations are operationally identified by the discriminator.

Compression is therefore not initially a chosen convenience.

It is forced whenever a discriminator has fewer available distinctions than the realization space to which it is coupled.

Deliberate abstraction, notation, measurement, classification, and modeling can then be understood as organized uses of this unavoidable identification.

This document develops that consequence. It treats fields as discriminator-relative availability relations, occasions as physically realized interaction opportunities, horizons as spans over which those opportunities remain supported, representations as physically realized compressions, and identity criteria as rules that determine which realization differences remain operative.

The result is not the slogan that everything is `just physics`.

The words `everything`, `just`, and `physics` each require clarification.

The result is a more limited structural claim:

> A bounded organization within reality cannot preserve more simultaneous distinctions than it can physically realize as distinct operative states.

---

## 1. The Modeler Is Inside the Modeled Reality

Suppose an organization \(A\) discriminates some variation in organization \(B\).

A common diagram suggests:

```text
reality
   ↓
observer
   ↓
representation
```

But the observer and representation do not arrive from outside the interaction.

A more explicit diagram is:

```text
reality

├── organization A
│   ├── sensory states
│   ├── memory states
│   ├── discriminations
│   └── representations
│
├── organization B
│   └── target variation
│
└── coupling processes
    └── propagation between A and B
```

The discriminator is one organized region of reality interacting with another.

Its observation is another event in reality.

Its internal state is another realized state.

Its model is another maintained organization.

No external viewpoint is required for discrimination to occur.

This changes the initial question.

Instead of asking:

> How does a representation outside reality correspond to reality?

one can ask:

> How does one realized organization preserve selected differences produced through its interaction with another realized organization?

---

## 2. A Physical Realization Premise

The following premise can be stated explicitly:

> Every event admitted as causally operative in the modeled reality is instantiated through some physical organization in that reality.

Call this the **physical realization premise**.

It includes:

```text
neural activity
spoken language
written equations
computer instructions
network messages
database records
legal decisions
institutional procedures
model parameters
tokens produced by an artificial system
```

The premise does not by itself establish:

```text
that every physical difference is modeled
that every description uses the vocabulary of physics
that one scale exhausts all other scales
that organizational identities disappear
that current physical theories are complete
that every possible reality must satisfy the premise
```

It states a realization dependency.

If a thought changes a hand movement, then some realized organization bridges the thought and movement.

If an equation on a blackboard changes a later calculation, then marks, light, sensory processing, memory, and action form part of the propagation chain.

If a legal status changes access to a building, then records, communications, recognition procedures, bodies, locks, and enforcement processes participate in the realization.

No example has been supplied in which a non-instantiated distinction enters the chain, changes its physical continuation, and does so without any realization through which the influence propagates.

## 3. What `All Just Physics` Compresses

The statement:

> It is all just physics.

contains several unresolved terms.

### What does `all` range over?

Possible ranges include:

```text
all observable events
all causally operative events
all entities admitted by one model
all possible entities
all of reality without remainder
```

These are not equivalent claims.

### What does `physics` denote?

It may denote:

```text
physical realization in general
currently accepted physical theories
fundamental-scale variables
laboratory measurement practices
one vocabulary used to describe physical organization
```

These are also not equivalent.

### What does `just` remove?

It may imply:

```text
no additional substances
no additional causal channels
no additional organizations
no additional descriptions
no additional identity criteria
no additional explanatory relations
```

Only some of these implications follow from a physical realization premise.

The word `just` merges them without identifying which elimination is claimed.

A less compressed statement is:

> The organizations and distinctions participating in the interaction are physically realized, while different descriptions may preserve different relations among their physical states.

This statement does not divide reality into physical and nonphysical portions.

It also does not require all physical states to be represented at once.

## 4. Shared Substrate Does Not Eliminate Organization

If several organizations are physically realized, they may share materials, energy flows, and surrounding conditions.

Shared substrate does not imply shared identity.

A whirlpool and the surrounding water are not composed from different substances.

Their distinction is organizational:

```text
flow pattern
boundary conditions
coupling to surroundings
persistence over a horizon
re-identification across material replacement
```

Likewise, a person does not require matter unavailable elsewhere in the environment.

The person remains distinguishable through an organization of:

```text
body boundaries
metabolic continuity
sensorimotor coupling
memory relations
causal history
social and legal re-identification
```

The claim that a person is physically realized therefore does not entail:

```text
the person is identical to every surrounding physical state
personal identity is absent
organizational boundaries are unreal
only microscopic descriptions remain admissible
```

Identity need not be grounded in a separate substance.

It can be grounded in maintained organization under a selected criterion.

## 5. Distinction Within a Shared Reality

Let \(R\) denote a larger realized organization and let \(A\subset R\) denote a discriminator organization.

The notation \(A\subset R\) does not mean that the boundary of \(A\) is final at every scale.

It records a cut under which some processes are attributed to \(A\) and others to its surroundings.

A discriminator can distinguish its own shape, state, or continuation from other organizations because interactions are not spatially or causally uniform.

For example:

```text
pressure differs across a skin boundary
light differs across retinal receptors
signals differ across a network interface
memory addresses differ across allocated regions
chemical concentrations differ across membranes
```

The discriminator's identity is therefore not eliminated by shared physical realization.

Its identity is expressed through a maintained asymmetry:

```text
states attributed here
versus
states attributed elsewhere
```

The distinction is contextual and physically constrained.

It need not be metaphysically absolute to be operational.

## 6. Field as Discriminator-Relative Availability

The word `field` can suggest an ultimate reservoir from which distinctions are simply given.

A more constrained definition is possible.

Let:

\[
U_{A,o}
\]

denote the field available to discriminator \(A\) on occasion \(o\).

This is not all reality.

It is not an independent source of truth.

It is the range of realized differences capable of becoming operative in \(A\) through the couplings active on that occasion.

Examples include:

```text
the photon distribution reaching a retina
voltages presented to an input circuit
bytes delivered to a parser
marks exposed to a camera
records returned by a query
pressure variations reaching a microphone
```

Availability is therefore relational:

\[
\operatorname{Available}(r,A,o,P),
\]

where:

```text
r = a realized variation
A = the discriminator organization
o = the interaction occasion
P = the maintained propagation path
```

A realized difference may exist without being available to a selected discriminator.

A radio signal can be present while no compatible receiver is coupled to it.

A file can remain on a disk while no powered path reads it.

A mark can remain on paper in darkness while no optical path reaches a discriminator.

Existence and availability are therefore different relations.

## 7. Availability as Propagated Operativity

A difference is actually available when it has propagated far enough through a realization chain to alter the operative state space of the discriminator.

A compact definition is:

> A realized difference is actually available to discriminator \(A\) on occasion \(o\) when an active physical propagation chain maps that difference into a state of \(A\) that can participate in a subsequent discrimination or action.

This can be written:

\[
r
\xrightarrow{P_o}
d_A,
\]

where \(P_o\) is the propagation realized on occasion \(o\), and \(d_A\) is an operative discriminator state.

The chain may include many transformations:

```text
optical
mechanical
electrical
chemical
neural
computational
```

These are not exits from physical realization.

They are different organizations and scales within it.

Availability is lost when the chain no longer supports the relevant propagation.

Examples:

```text
no electricity → no display emission
no display emission → no retinal stimulation
no retinal stimulation → no visual character discrimination

broken fiber → no received optical sequence
no received sequence → no decoded frame
no decoded frame → no token stream

failed metabolism → no maintained retinal response
no maintained response → no usable visual field
```

## 8. Physical, Computational, Logical, and Institutional Are Not Parallel Substrates

It is common to list:

```text
physical paths
computational paths
logical paths
institutional paths
```

as though each were a separate kind of carrier.

That description can hide the bridges on which the later organizations depend.

A more explicit stack is:

```text
material and energetic conditions
        ↓
electromagnetic or mechanical propagation
        ↓
circuit and device organization
        ↓
digital state organization
        ↓
protocol and instruction organization
        ↓
symbolic and inferential organization
        ↓
institutional recognition and action
```

Each later label selects relations preserved across many lower-level states.

The later organization remains physically realized.

A logical transition performed by a processor is not transmitted through a second nonphysical channel.

It is a description of relations maintained across processor states.

An institutional transition is not an event outside physical reality.

It is maintained through documents, servers, bodies, communications, recognition procedures, and actions.

The layers differ in their identity criteria and retained relations.

They do not require separate substances.

## 9. The Monitor as an Intermediate Discriminator

A monitor does not merely hold an image.

It transforms one organization of differences into another organization that can be discriminated by a later system.

A simplified chain is:

```text
program state
    ↓
rendering commands
    ↓
frame-buffer states
    ↓
display-controller signals
    ↓
pixel activation states
    ↓
emitted or transmitted photons
    ↓
retinal receptor states
    ↓
neural activity
    ↓
character discrimination
```

The monitor discriminates among electrical input configurations by producing different pixel states.

Its output distinctions matter only relative to a downstream coupling.

If distinct pixel states produce no differences that a downstream discriminator can receive, then those distinctions do not become available through that path.

This does not mean the monitor has no physical state.

It means the selected function of the monitor depends on a chain:

> preserve and transform differences into a form discriminable by a later organization.

A stack of discriminators can therefore be represented as:

\[
D_1\to D_2\to\cdots\to D_n,
\]

where each \(D_i\) maps distinctions available at one organization into distinctions available at the next.

Failure at an earlier stage constrains every later stage whose availability depends on it.

## 10. Potential and Actual Availability

Availability has at least two states.

### Potential availability

A physically realizable propagation path exists under the admitted constraints, but the relevant propagation is not currently occurring.

Examples:

```text
a powered camera can be pointed toward an object
an unread disk sector can be addressed
closed eyes can be opened
an inactive network route can be established
```

Write:

\[
\operatorname{Reachable}_{C}(r,A)
\]

when some admissible sequence of physical transitions could make realization \(r\) operative in \(A\).

### Actual availability

The propagation has occurred sufficiently for \(r\) to affect the present operative state of \(A\).

Write:

\[
\operatorname{Propagated}(r,A,o).
\]

Then:

\[
\operatorname{Propagated}(r,A,o)
\Longrightarrow
\operatorname{Reachable}_{C}(r,A),
\]

but the converse need not hold on the current occasion.

Potential availability describes constrained reachability.

Actual availability describes realized coupling.

## 11. Occasion as Interaction Opportunity

An occasion need not be supplied by a universal counter.

It need not be an inherent frequency of the field.

An occasion can be defined as:

> a bounded interaction event sufficient to present a new candidate difference to a discriminator or to update its operative state.

Examples include:

```text
a camera exposure
a completed sensor sample
a clocked processor transition
an asynchronous message arrival
a retinal integration interval
a database query response
a physical contact event
```

Let:

\[
o_1,o_2,\ldots
\]

denote successive interaction occasions.

Their succession may be established by:

```text
clock phases
causal ordering
message order
state transitions
completed measurements
recovery cycles
```

A periodic frequency is one possible mechanism for generating occasions.

It is not required by the general concept.

An asynchronous system can have occasions without a fixed frequency.

A retina can receive successive discriminable changes without storing an explicit iteration count.

The requirement is not a global clock.

It is a physically realized basis for distinguishing one interaction opportunity from another.

## 12. Maintained Availability Over a Horizon

One successful propagation does not establish continuing availability.

Let:

\[
H=\{o_1,\ldots,o_k\}
\]

be a selected horizon of interaction occasions.

A distinction class \(X\) is maintained as available to \(A\) over \(H\) when the realization network preserves the capacity for relevant members of \(X\) to propagate into operative states of \(A\) throughout the occasions required by the model.

This may be written:

\[
\operatorname{MaintainedAvailable}(X,A,H,P).
\]

The condition does not require identical signals on every occasion.

It requires continued support for the selected discrimination relation.

For vision, maintenance may depend on:

```text
metabolic energy
oxygen and glucose delivery
photoreceptor recovery
tear-film stability
neural integration
orientation and exposure
```

For computation, maintenance may depend on:

```text
electrical power
clock or event coordination
memory retention
cooling
error control
communication links
```

For institutional recognition, maintenance may depend on:

```text
records
identifiers
authorized interpreters
communication
procedural continuity
material access controls
```

The field available on each occasion is therefore not simply present.

It is recreated or preserved by a maintained network of couplings.

## 13. Permanence as an Unbounded Horizon Claim

A model may silently treat availability as permanent.

That amounts to replacing a declared horizon with an unrestricted one:

\[
H\to\infty.
\]

Such a statement suppresses questions about:

```text
resource exhaustion
component failure
metabolic interruption
signal loss
memory decay
institutional discontinuity
changes of scale
changes of coupling
```

A more explicit claim is:

> The selected availability relation is assumed to hold over horizon \(H\) under maintenance conditions \(M\).

This does not determine whether \(H\) is long or short.

It records that persistence is indexed.

A field available during one exposure need not remain available during the next.

A token retained within one context window need not remain available after the context is discarded.

A legal identity maintained under one institutional continuity need not survive the loss of the institution that re-identifies it.

## 14. Boundedness as Finite Distinguishability

The term `bounded` can refer to several limits:

```text
finite spatial extent
finite material resources
finite energy
finite interaction rate
finite memory
finite lifetime
finite state distinguishability
finite resolution
```

For the compression argument, the most direct limit is:

> A bounded discriminator can realize only some limited range of mutually distinguishable operative states over the selected occasion and horizon.

Let:

\[
D_{A,o}
\]

be the set of states that discriminator \(A\) can operationally distinguish on occasion \(o\).

The size of \(D_{A,o}\) need not be inferred from mass alone.

Two systems containing different amounts of matter may support different state capacities because of:

```text
organization
energy constraints
noise
coupling
resolution
stability
readout procedure
horizon
```

Therefore the relevant comparison is not simply:

```text
less matter versus more matter
```

but:

```text
fewer distinguishable operative states versus more distinguishable target states
```

Matter participates in realizing the capacity.

State capacity is the quantity used by the counting argument.

## 15. The Discriminator Mapping

Let:

\[
R_{B,o}
\]

be the target realization states of organization \(B\) relevant on occasion \(o\).

Let:

\[
D_{A,o}
\]

be the operative states available to discriminator \(A\).

The interaction induces a mapping:

\[
\Phi_{A,B,o}:R_{B,o}\to D_{A,o}.
\]

This mapping includes the entire active chain between target and discriminator:

```text
target variation
propagation
transduction
filtering
internal state transition
```

A discriminator distinguishes target states \(r_1\) and \(r_2\) on occasion \(o\) only if:

\[
\Phi_{A,B,o}(r_1)
eq\Phi_{A,B,o}(r_2).
\]

If:

\[
\Phi_{A,B,o}(r_1)=\Phi_{A,B,o}(r_2),
\]

then the target difference is not preserved in the discriminator state on that occasion.

The difference may remain physically present elsewhere.

It is unavailable as a distinction to that discriminator through that mapping.

## 16. Forced Compression

Suppose:

\[
|R_{B,o}|>|D_{A,o}|.
\]

Then no injective mapping from \(R_{B,o}\) into \(D_{A,o}\) exists.

Therefore there must be at least two distinct target realizations \(r_i\neq r_j\) such that:

\[
\Phi_{A,B,o}(r_i)=\Phi_{A,B,o}(r_j).
\]

The two realizations become operationally equivalent relative to \(A\), \(o\), and \(\Phi\).

Define:

\[
r_i\sim_{A,o}r_j
\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad
\Phi_{A,B,o}(r_i)=\Phi_{A,B,o}(r_j).
\]

The mapping partitions the target realization space into equivalence classes:

\[
R_{B,o}/\!\sim_{A,o}.
\]

Each class contains target states that the discriminator does not distinguish through the active interaction.

This is the elementary form of compression.

> Compression occurs whenever multiple distinguishable realization states map to one operative discriminator state.

No explicit compression algorithm is required.

No decision is required.

No preference is required.

The compression follows from the capacities and mapping.

---

## 17. The Photon Example

The statement:

> two photons collapse into one distinct state

can be sharpened.

The relevant objects are not necessarily two individual photons.

They are two distinct incoming physical configurations.

Let:

\[
r_1\neq r_2
\]

be two photon configurations differing in any combination of:

```text
photon count
wavelength
timing
polarization
spatial distribution
phase relations
```

If the receiving organization enters the same operative state for both:

\[
\Phi(r_1)=\Phi(r_2),
\]

then those input configurations are compressed relative to that receiver and occasion.

The receiver does not eliminate the physical difference everywhere.

It fails to preserve that difference in its own available state.

Another discriminator with a different coupling or resolution may separate them:

\[
\Psi(r_1)\neq\Psi(r_2).
\]

Compression is therefore discriminator-relative without being arbitrary.

The physical mapping constrains which differences merge.

## 18. Forced Compression and Deliberate Abstraction

Forced compression and deliberate abstraction should be distinguished.

### Forced compression

This occurs because the realized mapping is non-injective.

The discriminator cannot preserve all target distinctions through its available states.

### Deliberate abstraction

A later organization selects which already available distinctions to retain, merge, name, or use for a question.

For example, a temperature measurement may physically support several response differences, while a reporting procedure groups them into integer degrees.

The first loss may occur in the sensor coupling.

A second loss may occur in digitization.

A third may occur in display formatting.

A fourth may occur in human recollection.

Thus a realization chain can contain successive compressions:

\[
R
\xrightarrow{\Phi_1}
D_1
\xrightarrow{\Phi_2}
D_2
\xrightarrow{\Phi_3}
D_3.
\]

Each mapping induces its own equivalence classes.

A later abstraction cannot recover distinctions that no longer propagate unless another path preserves them.

## 19. Representation Is Itself Realized

A representation is not outside the physical organization it represents.

It is another realized organization participating in further interaction.

Examples:

```text
ink patterns on paper
pixel states on a display
magnetic or electrical memory states
sound-pressure patterns
neural activity
processor and accelerator states
```

A representation relation can be written:

\[
\operatorname{Rep}_{C}(m,r),
\]

where \(m\) is a realized modeling organization and \(r\) is a realized target organization.

The relation does not make \(m\) nonphysical.

It states that variations in \(m\) are used to preserve, predict, control, or re-identify selected variations in \(r\).

The representation itself consumes:

```text
material
energy
space
time
maintenance
coupling capacity
```

A full account of modeling must therefore include the model as part of the modeled reality.

## 20. Representation Does Not Specify Every State of Its Realization

A realized representation has more physical detail than the distinctions it presents under a selected interpretation.

The inscription:

```text
x = x + 1
```

may vary in:

```text
ink chemistry
pixel brightness
font
position
paper fibers
voltage levels
timing
thermal state
```

while remaining one expression under the active recognition criterion.

The representation relation therefore groups many physical realizations as equivalent:

\[
m_1\equiv_C m_2.
\]

Likewise, one source-level program can be realized by many binaries, and one binary execution can be realized by many lower-level trajectories.

The statement:

> representation is not realization

is too compressed if it suggests that representations are not physically realized.

A more explicit statement is:

> A representation is physically realized, while its interpreted structure does not preserve every physical distinction present in that realization.

This is another application of compression.

## 21. Source, Binary, and Gate Trajectory

Consider the chain:

```text
source code
    ↓
compiler transformations
    ↓
intermediate representation
    ↓
machine instructions
    ↓
micro-operations
    ↓
logic-gate transitions
    ↓
transistor and charge trajectories
```

Every stage is physically realized when it is present or executed.

The stages are not identical state descriptions.

A source statement may correspond to multiple binaries because of:

```text
compiler version
optimization
instruction set
register allocation
linking
runtime environment
```

A binary instruction may correspond to multiple microarchitectural trajectories because of:

```text
pipeline state
cache state
branch prediction
scheduling
speculation
hardware implementation
```

A gate-level transition may correspond to multiple lower-level physical microstates because the selected logic criterion identifies ranges of voltages and timings as the same logical state.

Thus:

\[
\text{one higher-level state}
\longleftrightarrow
\text{many admissible lower-level realizations}.
\]

The higher-level organization is not nonphysical.

It is an equivalence class over physical realizations under a selected operational criterion.

## 22. Identity Criteria as Compression Rules

An identity criterion determines which realization differences are ignored for re-identification.

Let \(I\) be an identity relation over realizations:

\[
r_1\equiv_I r_2.
\]

Then \(r_1\) and \(r_2\) count as the same modeled unit or state under \(I\), even if they differ physically in ways not retained by the model.

Examples:

```text
same character despite different pixels
same variable despite different values
same person despite material replacement
same program despite different memory addresses
same legal document despite different storage media
same logical value across a voltage tolerance range
```

Identity does not eliminate the lower-level differences.

It organizes them into equivalence classes for later operations.

A model can therefore be described as a physically realized organization operating over selected equivalence classes induced by:

```text
its coupling
its resolution
its state capacity
its identity criteria
its horizon
```

## 23. Can a Smaller Organization Reflect a Larger One?

The phrase:

> less matter cannot reflect more matter

captures an intuition but does not yet provide the formal condition.

Mass, volume, or particle count alone does not determine the number of operationally distinguishable states.

A direct statement uses state capacity.

Let the target system have \(M\) distinguishable states:

\[
|R|=M.
\]

Let the representing system have \(N\) distinguishable internal states available for the representation:

\[
|D|=N.
\]

For an exact internal representation that distinguishes every possible target state at one occasion, an injective encoding is required:

\[
e:R\hookrightarrow D.
\]

Such an encoding exists only if:

\[
N\ge M.
\]

If:

\[
N<M,
\]

then exact simultaneous internal reflection is impossible under those state definitions.

At least two target states must share one representing state.

This is not a statement that the smaller organization can say nothing about the larger one.

It can preserve selected relations, query portions over time, predict limited variables, or use external storage.

The claim concerns full state discrimination in the representing organization alone.

---

## 24. Sequential Description Does Not Remove the Capacity Requirement

A smaller system may generate a long sequence of symbols over time.

This can appear to represent a target with more states than the system can hold at once.

But the sequence itself is physically realized through:

```text
external memory
successive output states
recorded history
an observer retaining earlier symbols
the target being queried repeatedly
```

The effective representing organization is then not only the instantaneous internal state.

It is the composite:

\[
D_{\text{composite}}
=
D_{\text{internal}}
\times
D_{\text{external}}
\times
D_{\text{history}}
\times\cdots
\]

The additional distinguishability is physically carried somewhere.

If no external trace, memory, or retained succession exists, then earlier output differences are unavailable to later comparison.

Time does not create free storage.

A temporal representation uses physically distinguishable events and maintained relations among them.

---

## 25. Full Self-Reflection

A system may model itself.

The counting argument does not prohibit all self-models.

It constrains a particular demand:

> a separate, simultaneously available, full-resolution internal state that uniquely specifies every distinguishable state of the entire system, including the representing state itself.

Let \(A\) be the full system and \(D\subset A\) the region allocated to its internal model.

If \(D\) has fewer distinguishable states than \(A\), then \(D\) cannot injectively encode all states of \(A\).

If the representation uses the rest of \(A\) as part of the encoding, then the representation is distributed through the system rather than contained in a smaller internal copy.

If the target state itself serves as its own representation, no separate reflective copy has been produced.

If the model records only selected variables, it is compressed rather than full-resolution.

Thus self-modeling remains possible through:

```text
partial models
predictive summaries
local state estimates
external traces
distributed representations
iterative measurements
```

The excluded case is not self-reference in general.

It is a smaller, fully independent copy preserving every state distinction of the larger system at once.

## 26. Finite Capacity to Realize Distinctions

Boundedness can now be stated more precisely:

> An organization is bounded relative to a modeling task when the physically realizable distinctions available to it over the selected horizon are fewer than, or otherwise insufficient to preserve, all target distinctions invoked by that task.

This includes more than memory size.

A distinction must be:

```text
physically realized
stable enough over the required interval
reachable through an active coupling
separable from noise under the readout regime
addressable for later use
maintained across required occasions
```

A nominal state that cannot be reliably entered, preserved, or read does not contribute the same operational capacity as a maintained distinguishable state.

Finite capacity is therefore a property of the realized organization and its interaction regime, not merely an abstract count written on paper.

## 27. Modeling as Organized Use of Equivalence Classes

Once compression is forced, modeling can be described as the organization of the resulting equivalence classes.

A modeling process may:

```text
select which classes receive names
refine or merge classes
assign addresses
record relations among classes
predict transitions among classes
evaluate claims using class-level criteria
reopen a class at another resolution
```

Let:

\[
\pi:R\to R/\!\sim
\]

be the projection from realization states to equivalence classes induced by a discriminator or identity criterion.

A model \(M\) can then operate over:

\[
M=(R/\!\sim,\mathcal R,\mathcal O,\mathcal E,H),
\]

where:

```text
R/~ = retained classes of realization
R   = relations among classes
O   = operations or transformations
E   = evaluation relations
H   = horizon over which the organization is maintained
```

The model does not need to contain an independent miniature of every target microstate.

It contains realized distinctions corresponding to selected classes and relations.

## 28. No Privileged Vocabulary Follows

The physical realization premise does not itself designate one vocabulary as universally sufficient.

That would require an additional claim about which distinctions are to be preserved.

A description in terms of:

```text
voltages
instructions
characters
contracts
organisms
```

selects different equivalence classes over the same or overlapping realization networks.

The neutral question is not:

> Which vocabulary is right?

It is:

> Which distinctions does this vocabulary preserve, which does it merge, through which realization path, for which question and horizon?

A voltage description may preserve differences ignored by a character description.

A character description may preserve relations not explicitly represented in the voltage variables selected by another model.

Both descriptions are physically realized when written, computed, or used.

Their difference lies in their cuts and equivalence relations.

## 29. Descriptive Language and Hidden Normativity

Terms such as:

```text
right
wrong
best
natural
mere
just
complete
fundamental
```

can introduce evaluation criteria without declaring them.

For example:

> Physics is not the right representation.

leaves unstated:

```text
right for which operation
right for which discriminator
right according to which loss function
right over which horizon
right relative to which retained distinctions
```

A descriptive replacement is:

> The selected physical-variable representation does not preserve every distinction invoked by model \(M\).

Likewise:

> This is only an abstraction.

can be replaced by:

> This organization maps multiple target realizations to the same modeled state.

The second formulation identifies a mapping property.

It does not evaluate the compression before an evaluation criterion has been supplied.

## 30. A Realization Stack

The developing dependency structure can be summarized as:

```text
physical constraints and resources
            ↓
maintained organization
            ↓
realized propagation paths
            ↓
interaction occasions
            ↓
discriminator-relative availability
            ↓
operative discrimination
            ↓
forced equivalence classes
            ↓
individuation and identity criteria
            ↓
addressable loci
            ↓
relations and operations
            ↓
evaluation and modeling
```

This is not necessarily a temporal sequence in every system.

Several layers can be mutually supporting.

The stack expresses dependency:

- no availability without a realized coupling;
- no repeated availability without maintenance over occasions;
- no discrimination without distinguishable discriminator states;
- no full discrimination when target distinctions exceed discriminator capacity;
- no reusable model unit without some identity and addressability relation.

## 31. A Compact Formal Schema

Let a realized modeling arrangement be:

\[
\mathfrak A
=
(A,B,P,O,H,\Phi,I,L,M),
\]

where:

```text
A = discriminator organization
B = target organization
P = physical propagation and transformation network
O = interaction occasions
H = selected horizon of occasions
Phi = mapping from target states to discriminator states
I = identity and equivalence criteria
L = addressable loci formed from retained distinctions
M = maintenance conditions supporting the arrangement
```

A minimal availability condition is:

\[
\operatorname{Available}_{\mathfrak A}(r,o)
\]

only if:

1. \(r\) is physically realized in the target arrangement on occasion \(o\);
2. an active path in \(P\) propagates some effect of \(r\) toward \(A\);
3. the propagated effect enters an operative state in \(A\);
4. that state remains usable for the discrimination required by the current operation.

A minimal exact-reflection condition is:

\[
\operatorname{ExactReflect}_{\mathfrak A}(R,D)
\]

only if the induced mapping:

\[
\Phi:R\to D
\]

is injective over the target states claimed to be distinguished.

If it is not injective, compression has occurred relative to the arrangement.

## 33. Central Principles

### Embedded Discriminator Principle

> A discriminator and its representations are realized organizations within the same reality in which their targets and interactions occur.

### Shared Substrate Principle

> Shared physical realization does not eliminate organizational identity; identity can be maintained through boundaries, couplings, histories, and re-identification criteria.

### Realized Influence Principle

> A difference becomes causally operative through a physically realized propagation chain rather than by entering interaction from an uninstantiated external domain.

### Relational Availability Principle

> Availability is not existence alone; it is the capacity of a realized difference to become operative in a selected discriminator through an active or reachable coupling.

### Layered Bridge Principle

> Computational, symbolic, logical, and institutional organizations are physically bridged layers of realization rather than independent causal substrates.

### Occasion Principle

> An occasion is a physically distinguishable interaction opportunity or completed update, not necessarily a tick of a universal clock or an inherent frequency of a field.

### Maintained Availability Principle

> Repeated availability over a horizon requires continued support of the propagation, transduction, discrimination, and re-identification capacities used by the model.

### Finite Distinguishability Principle

> A bounded discriminator can preserve only the distinctions it can physically realize as mutually distinguishable operative states under the selected conditions.

### Forced Compression Principle

> If the target realization space contains more distinguishable states than the discriminator state space, then multiple target states necessarily map to one discriminator state.

### Discriminator-Relative Compression Principle

> A distinction compressed by one organization may remain available to another organization with a different coupling, resolution, or state capacity.

### Realized Representation Principle

> A representation is itself physically realized, while its interpreted organization generally identifies many physical realizations as equivalent.

### Exact Reflection Principle

> Exact simultaneous reflection of a target state space requires an injective mapping into a representing state space with at least equal operational distinguishability.

### Composite Capacity Principle

> When a representation uses external memory, retained history, environmental structure, or the target itself, the effective capacity belongs to the composite realization rather than to the local internal state alone.

### Indexed Evaluation Principle

> Evaluative terms become operational only after the question, criterion, discriminator, and horizon relative to which they apply have been specified.

## 34. Central Statements

> The observer does not leave reality in order to observe it.

> A model is another realized organization within the realization it models.

> Physical realization does not erase identity; it supplies a shared substrate across differently maintained organizations.

> Existence does not guarantee availability.

> Availability requires a realized path through which a difference can become operative in a discriminator.

> A field is not all reality; it is the discriminator-relative frontier of currently propagated or reachable distinctions.

> An occasion is an interaction opportunity made distinct by a physical update, event, or causal succession.

> Frequency can generate occasions, but occasion does not require fixed frequency.

> Continued observation is maintained, not given.

> A monitor, network, retina, parser, and interpreter can form a stack in which each organization transforms distinctions for a later discriminator.

> Logical and institutional distinctions do not require nonphysical carriers; they are maintained organizations over physically propagated states.

> A bounded discriminator has a bounded space of operative distinctions.

> Whenever two target realizations produce the same operative discriminator state, those realizations are compressed relative to that discriminator.

> If the target has more distinguishable states than the discriminator, compression is forced.

> A representation is physically realized but does not preserve every physical distinction of its own realization or of its target.

> Source code, machine code, and gate trajectories are connected realizations at different equivalence resolutions, not one identical state description.

> Identity criteria specify which physical differences are ignored for the current re-identification.

> Less matter does not by itself imply less state capacity; the formal limit concerns operationally distinguishable states.

> A smaller state space cannot contain an injective simultaneous encoding of a larger state space.

> Sequential description uses physically realized history, output, or external memory; the additional capacity is carried by the composite system.

> Compression is not initially an error, preference, or convenience. It is a structural consequence of non-injective realization mappings.

> Modeling begins when forced equivalence classes are organized into addressable distinctions, relations, operations, and evaluations.

## 35. Conclusion

A discriminator need not be placed outside reality.

It can be treated as one physical organization among others.

Its identity is not removed by that placement.

Identity can persist through maintained organization even when substrate is shared, exchanged, or transformed.

The central modeling problem then becomes concrete.

A target difference must propagate through a maintained physical chain.

The chain must produce an operative difference in the discriminator.

Successive discriminations require physically distinguishable occasions.

Continued availability requires maintenance over a selected horizon.

The discriminator can preserve only the distinctions supported by its realized state capacity, coupling, resolution, and stability.

When the target realization space exceeds that capacity, the induced mapping cannot remain injective.

Multiple target states become one discriminator state.

Compression follows.

Representations, symbols, measurements, source code, logical relations, and institutional identities do not escape this realization structure. They are maintained physical organizations whose active criteria preserve some relations while merging others.

The result is not that identity disappears into a substrate.

The substrate is shared; the organizations remain distinguishable.

Nor is the result that every event must be written in an unlimited physical vocabulary.

A bounded organization cannot realize an unlimited full-resolution account. Its models are themselves finite physical organizations formed through forced and organized compression.

A compact statement is:

\[
\boxed{
\text{embedded realization}
+
\text{finite distinguishability}
\Longrightarrow
\text{forced compression}
}
\]

The modeling question is therefore not how a representation leaves physical reality.

It is:

> Which realized differences can propagate into this bounded organization, which distinctions survive the mapping, which are merged, and over which horizon can the resulting organization remain available?
